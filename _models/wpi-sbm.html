Setting

Let $\Omega:=\{\pm 1\}^N$ be the hypercube. We consider the following 2-community SBM: Let $d$ be a fixed average degree and $\eps \in(0,1)$. Let further $x\sim \text{Unif}(\Omega)$. Then, $G\sim \text{SBM}(N,d,\eps)$ is a random graph where for all $i< j$: $$ \P(ij\in E)=\frac{d}{N}(1+\eps x_ix_j) $$ Let $A$ be its adjacency matrix. Then, the signal-to-noise (SNR) ratio is defined as $\lambda:= \eps^2d$. It is known that for 2-community SBM, weak recovery is possible if and only if $\lambda > 1$, also known as the KS threshold.

Exact Bayes Posterior of SBM

The posterior of $x$ given $G$ is given by the Ising model: $$ \mu(\sigma)\propto \exp\left( \frac{\arctanh \eps+\kappa}{2}\sigma^\top A\sigma -\frac{\kappa}{2}\langle 1,\sigma\rangle ^2\right) $$ i.e. the interaction matrix is given by $(\arctanh \eps+\kappa) A - \kappa 11^\top$ where $\kappa := \frac{1}{2}\log \frac{1-(1-\eps)d / N}{1-(1+\eps)d / N}$. Indeed, the posteror is given by: $$ p(x | G)\propto p (G | x) = \prod_{i\sim j }\frac{d}{N}(1+\eps x_i x_j)\cdot \prod_{i\not\sim j}\left(1 - \frac{d}{N}(1+\eps x_ix_j)\right) $$ Then, for $s\in \{\pm 1\}$, $\log (1+\eps s) = \frac{1}{2}\log (1-\eps^2) + s\cdot \arctanh \eps$. Similarly, $\log (1-\frac{d}{N}(1+\eps s)) = - \kappa s + \text{const}$. Therefore, $$ \log p(x|G) \propto \sum_{i\sim j}(x_ix_j \arctanh\eps) + \sum_{i\not\sim j} x_i x_j \kappa $$ It follows that: $$ \mu(\sigma)\propto \exp\left(\arctanh \eps \sum_{i\sim j}\sigma_i \sigma_j -\kappa \sum_{i< j} (1-A_{ij}) \sigma_i\sigma_j\right) $$

Two-community SBM. For $\beta\gt 0$ let $$ \mu_\beta(\sigma)\propto \exp\Big(\beta\sum_{i\lt j}J_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j\Big),\qquad t:=\tanh(\beta J_e),\qquad x:=d\,t^2 . $$

Cavity heuristics: the equilibrium of $\mu_\beta$ is informative iff $d\,\omega t\gt 1$, i.e. $x\gt 1/\lambda$, and the noise part is non-glassy iff $x\lt 1$. So the window $x\in(1/\lambda,1)$ is non-empty for every $\lambda\gt 1$. (Bayes is $x=\lambda\gt 1$: always glassy noise. The proven high-temperature range of Liu–Mohanty–Rajaraman–Wu is $x\le 0.03$, which forces $\lambda\gtrsim 35$.) I use the balanced point where the informative margin $\sqrt{\lambda x}-1$ equals the glassy margin $1-x$: $$ x_\star=\tfrac12\Big((4+\lambda)-\sqrt{(4+\lambda)^2-16}\Big),\qquad \text{margins}=0.37,\ 0.24,\ 0.14,\ 0.09\ \text{ for }\lambda=3,\ 2,\ 1.5,\ 1.3 . $$

Dynamics: continuous-time heat-bath Glauber for $\mu_\beta$, one clock of rate one per site (time is measured in sweeps): $$ Lf(\sigma)=\sum_i r_i(\sigma)\big(f(\sigma^{i})-f(\sigma)\big),\qquad r_i=\tfrac12\big(1-\sigma_i\tanh(\beta h_i)\big),\qquad h_i=\sum_j J_{ij}\sigma_j , $$ $P_s=e^{sL}$. The overlap is $m(\sigma)=\langle x^*,\sigma\rangle/n$ and $v=m^2$. The start is uniform, $\nu_0$, and the output is $X_U$ with $U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]$.

2. Reduction theorem (rigorous)

Theorem 1. Let $\psi:\Omega\to[0,\psi_{\max}]$, $\alpha\ge 0$, $0\le s_1\lt s_2$, $c\in(0,1]$ and $D\subseteq\Omega$. Suppose that for every $x\in D$ $$ \E_x\Big[\frac{1}{s_2-s_1}\int_{s_1}^{s_2} v(X_r)\,dr\Big]+\frac{\alpha}{s_2-s_1}\,\E_x\big[\psi(X_{s_1})-\psi(X_{s_2})\big]\ \ge\ c. \tag{$\star$} $$ Then for every initial law $\nu$ and every $T\gt 0$, $$ \E_\nu\big[v(X_U)\big]\ \ge\ c\,\mathbb P_\nu(\tau_{D^c}\gt T)\;-\;\frac{\alpha\,\psi_{\max}+(s_1+s_2)/2}{T}, $$ where $\tau_{D^c}$ is the first exit time from $D$.

Proof. Put $w=s_2-s_1$, $K=\frac1w\int_{s_1}^{s_2}P_r\,dr$, and $\omega(r)=1$ on $[0,s_1]$, $\omega(r)=(s_2-r)/w$ on $[s_1,s_2]$. Define $$ f=\alpha K\psi+\int_0^{s_2}\omega(r)\,P_r(1-v)\,dr . $$ Since $\psi\ge 0$ and $v\le 1$, $0\le f\le \alpha\psi_{\max}+\int_0^{s_2}\omega=\alpha\psi_{\max}+(s_1+s_2)/2$. Using $LP_r=\frac{d}{dr}P_r$, $LK\psi=(P_{s_2}\psi-P_{s_1}\psi)/w$, and integrating by parts, $L\int_0^{s_2}\omega P_r\phi\,dr=-\phi+\frac1w\int_{s_1}^{s_2}P_r\phi\,dr=K\phi-\phi$. With $\phi=1-v$, $$ Lf=\frac{\alpha}{w}\big(P_{s_2}\psi-P_{s_1}\psi\big)+v-Kv , $$ so $(\star)$ is exactly $Lf\le v-c$ on $D$. Let $\tau=T\wedge\tau_{D^c}$. Dynkin's formula and $f\ge 0$ give $c\,\E\tau\le \E\int_0^\tau v(X_r)dr+\E f(X_0)\le \E\int_0^T v(X_r)dr+\alpha\psi_{\max}+(s_1+s_2)/2$, and $\E\tau\ge T\,\mathbb P(\tau_{D^c}\gt T)$. Divide by $T$. $\square$

Remarks. (i) Nothing about reversibility, $\mu_\beta$, its partition function or entropy is used; the price is $\psi_{\max}$, not $\mathrm{KL}(\nu_0\Vert\mu_\beta)\approx 0.2n$ (which is what the WPI route through entropy pays). (ii) Letting $s_2\downarrow s_1=s$ gives the pointwise form $\E_x[v(X_s)-\alpha L\psi(X_s)]\ge c$; $s=0$ is dual feasibility in the flow LP with potential $\alpha\psi$. $s_1=0$ gives the integrated form. The window $[s_1,s_2]$ combines a burn-in (to skip the fast initial transient) with time-averaging (which telescopes the noise of $\psi$). Numerically only the windowed form works near the threshold (Section 5). (iii) The multiplicative / WPI certificate is a special case: if $h\ge 1$ and $-Lh/h+\theta v\ge\kappa$ on $D$, then $\psi=\log h$, $\alpha=1/\theta$, $s_1=s_2=0$ satisfies $(\star)$ with $c=\kappa/\theta$, because $L\log h\le Lh/h$.

Corollary 2. If $(\star)$ holds, $\delta:=\mathbb P_{\nu_0}(\tau_{D^c}\le T)\le 1/4$ and $T\ge 4\big(\alpha\psi_{\max}+(s_1+s_2)/2\big)/c$, then $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c/2$ and $\mathbb P\big(|m(X_U)|\ge \sqrt{c/4}\big)\ge c/4$. Proof. $c(1-\delta)-c/4\ge c/2$; and for $w=m^2\in[0,1]$, $\E w\le \mathbb P(w\ge \E w/2)+\E w/2$. $\square$

3. The potential

Let $u$ be the informative eigenvector of the Bethe Hessian at the chain's temperature, $H(t)=I+\frac{t^2}{1-t^2}D_G-\frac{t}{1-t^2}A_G$ (or the vertex part of the non-backtracking eigenvector; both work numerically), normalized $\Vert u\Vert_2^2=n$. Put $y(\sigma)=\langle u,\sigma\rangle/n$ and $$ \psi(\sigma)=\ell\big(y^2\big),\qquad \ell(q)=\log\frac{z^2+\eta}{q+\eta}\ \ (q\le z^2),\qquad \eta=\frac{c_\eta}{n}, $$ with $\ell$ continued smoothly to $0$ beyond $z^2$ for the proofs (the numerics use the kink $\ell_+$). Then $\psi_{\max}=\log(1+z^2 n/c_\eta)=\log n+O(1)$, so Corollary 2 needs $T=O(\log n)$ sweeps, i.e. $O(n\log n)$ single-site updates, as soon as $(\star)$ holds with $n$-independent $\alpha,c,s_1,s_2,c_\eta,z$.

Lemma 3 (second-order expansion). Let $\delta_i=-2u_i\sigma_i/n$ be the change of $y$ when spin $i$ flips, and $$ b(\sigma):=Ly=\frac1n\sum_i u_i\big(\tanh(\beta h_i)-\sigma_i\big),\qquad q(\sigma):=\sum_i r_i\delta_i^2=\frac{2}{n^2}\sum_i u_i^2\big(1-\sigma_i\tanh(\beta h_i)\big). $$ For $\psi=\ell(y^2)$ with $\ell$ smooth, $L\psi=\psi'(y)\,b+\tfrac12\psi''(y)\,q+\rho$ with $|\rho|\le\frac43\Vert\psi'''\Vert_\infty\Vert u\Vert_\infty/n^2$.

Proof. $r_i\delta_i=\frac{u_i}{n}(\tanh\beta h_i-\sigma_i)$ gives the first-order term. Taylor's theorem leaves $\frac16\Vert\psi'''\Vert_\infty\sum_i r_i|\delta_i|^3\le\frac16\Vert\psi'''\Vert_\infty\cdot\frac{8}{n^3}\Vert u\Vert_\infty\sum_i u_i^2$. $\square$

For the log potential $\Vert\psi'''\Vert_\infty\lesssim\eta^{-3/2}$, so $|\rho|\lesssim \Vert u\Vert_\infty/(c_\eta^{3/2}\sqrt n)\to 0$ when $\Vert u\Vert_\infty=o(\sqrt n)$. In the scaled variables $\bar y=\sqrt n\,y$, $\bar b=\sqrt n\,b$, $\bar q=\frac{n}{2}q=\frac1n\sum_i u_i^2(1-\sigma_i\tanh\beta h_i)$, for $|y|\lt z$: $$ -L\psi=\frac{2\,\bar y\,\bar b}{\bar y^2+c_\eta}+\frac{2\,\bar q\,(c_\eta-\bar y^2)}{(\bar y^2+c_\eta)^2}+o(1). \tag{$\diamond$} $$ The first term is the drift (good when $\bar y\bar b\gt 0$, i.e. $|y|$ grows); the second is diffusion (good inside the flat region $\bar y^2\lt c_\eta$, bad outside).

Lemma 4 (uniform diffusion floor). For every $\sigma$, $\bar q(\sigma)\ge \frac1n\sum_i u_i^2\, e^{-2\beta(J_e\deg_i+|J_n|n)}$.

Proof. $1-\sigma\tanh(\beta h)\ge 1-\tanh(\beta|h|)=\frac{2}{1+e^{2\beta|h|}}\ge e^{-2\beta|h|}$ and $|h_i|\le J_e\deg_i+|J_n|(n-1)$. $\square$

So $\bar q$ is bounded below uniformly over configurations (worst case) once $u$ puts a constant fraction of its mass on vertices of bounded degree ($|J_n|n\to \frac{a-b}{2}$ is $O(1)$). Measured: $\bar q\in[0.23,0.43]$ on every configuration examined.

Heuristic size of the drift. If, after averaging out fast noise, $\bar b\approx\gamma\bar y$ with growth rate $\gamma\gt 0$, then $(\diamond)$ becomes $\varphi(\bar y^2)$ with $\varphi(w)=\frac{2\gamma w}{w+c_\eta}+\frac{2\bar q(c_\eta-w)}{(w+c_\eta)^2}$. For $c_\eta=2\bar q/\gamma$, writing $w=c_\eta r$, $\varphi/\gamma=\frac{2r^2+r+1}{(1+r)^2}$, minimized at $r=1/3$ with value $7/8$. So $-L\psi\ge \frac78\gamma$ throughout $|y|\lt z$, and Corollary 2 then needs $T\approx \frac{32}{7}\frac{\log n}{\gamma}$ sweeps, the observed escape time scale. Empirically $\gamma\approx 0.4\,|\lambda_{\min}(H(t))|$ roughly in all four settings ($\gamma\approx 0.084,\ 0.04,\ 0.017,\ 0.004$–$0.008$ per sweep for $\lambda=3,2,1.5,1.3$). The flat-region condition $c_\eta\gtrsim 2\bar q/\gamma$ is necessary for the pointwise form; with windowing the noise is averaged and small $c_\eta$ already works at $\lambda\ge 2$.

4. The main lemma (open) and the conditional theorem

Let $B=\{\sigma:\ |y(\sigma)|\ge z,\ m(\sigma)^2\lt c_1\}$ ("aligned with $u$ but not with $x^*$") and $D=\Omega\setminus B$.

Main Lemma (conjecture). Fix $d$, $\lambda\gt 1$ and $t$ at the balanced point. With high probability over $G$ there are $\alpha,c,c_1,s_1,s_2,c_\eta,z\gt 0$ independent of $n$ such that $(\star)$ holds for all $x\in D$.

Alignment Lemma (conjecture). $\mathbb P_{\nu_0}(\tau_B\le C\log n)\le 1/4$.

Theorem 5 (conditional). Under the two conjectures, Glauber for $\mu_\beta$ started uniformly and stopped at a uniform time in $[0,T]$, $T=C'\log n$ sweeps, satisfies $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c/2$; i.e. weak recovery in $O(n\log n)$ single-site updates, for every $\lambda\gt 1$. Proof: Theorem 1, Corollary 2 and $\psi_{\max}=\log n+O(1)$. $\square$

What the Main Lemma needs, and status.

  • (S1) Spectral input: $u$ correlates with $x^*$, $\Vert u\Vert_\infty=o(\sqrt n)$, and $u$ is delocalized over bounded-degree vertices. Expected from non-backtracking theory (Bordenave–Lelarge–Massoulié; Stephan–Massoulié) for the non-backtracking vector; for $H(t)$ via Ihara–Bass. Technical.
  • (S2) Expansion: Lemma 3, proved.
  • (S3) Diffusion floor: Lemma 4, proved given (S1).
  • (S4) Window growth (the core): for $|y(x)|\lt z$, $\frac{\alpha}{w}\E_x[\psi(X_{s_1})-\psi(X_{s_2})]\ge c$. By $(\diamond)$ this is a statement about the drift of $y$ along the window after a burn-in $s_1$. Suggested route: (a) finite speed of propagation for Glauber on sparse graphs (in $O(s_2)$ sweeps a vertex's trajectory depends w.h.p. only on an $O(s_2)$-neighbourhood, by disagreement percolation since rates are bounded and degrees are Poisson); (b) $u$ is approximately a local functional of the graph (non-backtracking eigenvectors are approximated by tree functionals); (c) this reduces the expected change of $y$ to a computation on Galton–Watson trees with couplings $\pm\operatorname{atanh} t$, where $x\lt 1$ should give contraction of the non-informative part and $\sqrt{\lambda x}\gt 1$ growth along $u$; (d) concentration of $\psi(y_{s_2})-\psi(y_{s_1})$ over the window (this is where windowing helps: the noise telescopes).
  • (S5) Alignment: open. Along every trajectory examined, $|m|\approx(0.5\text{–}0.9)\,|y|$ once $|y|$ is macroscopic.
  • (S6) Stability beyond the cutoff: on $\{|y|\ge z,\ m^2\ge c_1\}$ the window average of $v$ stays $\ge c$. Open, expected near equilibrium.

5. Numerical evidence

Setup: $d=5$, balanced window temperature, two graphs per setting. Configurations $x$ are sampled from five families: cold-start trajectories, $m\approx 0$ states pinned at the hot and at the Bayes temperature (then released), states pinned at half the equilibrium overlap, and equilibrium states with a 20% graph ball flipped. For each $x$, 24–32 independent replicas are run and the left side of $(\star)$ is estimated on the same replica paths; $c^*$ is the minimum over all sampled $x$. Parameters $(s_1,s_2,z,c_\eta,\alpha)$ are chosen on one graph and evaluated on the other ("cross-graph").

Earlier variants on the same data: the pointwise form ($s_1=s_2$) certifies 0.31 ($\lambda=3$) and 0.06–0.10 ($\lambda=2$) but fails at $\lambda=1.5$, $n=20$k, because the per-configuration noise of $L\psi(X_s)$ is as large as the signal $\approx 2\gamma$; the integrated form ($s_1=0$) fails because it averages over the fast initial transient, during which $|y|$ shrinks. The multiplicative certificate ($h_s=e^{-sH_\theta}h_0$) gave the same qualitative picture with worse constants.

Supporting measurements: $\bar q\ge 0.23$ on all configurations (Lemma 4); $\gamma\approx 0.4|\lambda_{\min}(H(t))|$; alignment $|m|/|y|\approx 0.5$–$0.9$; every adversarial start escapes (no traps found in any family).

6. Caveats and next steps

  • $(\star)$ is checked on sampled configurations (a few hundred to a thousand per setting), not on all of $D$; the minimum of noisy replica means is biased downward, so the certified values are conservative in that respect, but the sampling of $x$ is not exhaustive.
  • Two graphs per setting; potential parameters are tuned (the cross-graph column is the honest number).
  • Next: prove (S4) on trees first (step (c)), which is a finite-dimensional recursion; then the locality steps (a)–(b). In parallel: $n$-scaling of the windowed certificate at $\lambda=1.3$ (at $\lambda=1.5$ it improves from $n=20$k to $80$k), and longer windows at $\lambda=1.3$ (the best window there is the longest one recorded).
