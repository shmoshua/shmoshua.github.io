Setup

For $\sigma \in \{\pm 1\}^n$ and i.i.d. couplings $g_{ij} \sim \mathcal N(0,1)$, the Gibbs measure at inverse temperature $\beta$ is $$ \mu_\beta(\sigma) \propto \exp\Big( \frac{\beta}{\sqrt n} \sum_{i< j} g_{ij}\, \sigma_i \sigma_j \Big). $$

Results

Inverse Temperature Thresholds

The replica symmetric phase can be split up to the following: $$ 0\le \beta_{\text{unique}}\le \beta_{\text{SL}}\le \beta_{\text{shatter}} \le \beta_{\text{RS}} $$ where:
  1. Uniqueness threshold: Below $\beta_{\text{unique}}$, Glauber dynamics satisfy the Poincare inequality w.h.p. Hence, we have fast mixing under worst case initialization. Worst case mixing is exponentially slow above this threshold.
  2. Stochastic Localization threshold: Below $\beta_{\text{SL}}$, algorithmic stochastic localization successfully samples from $\mu$. Above this threshold, it fails. Additionally, below this threshold, simulated annealing is proven to sample, though it is not known whether it fails above $\beta_{\text{SL}}$.
  3. Shattering threshold: Below $\beta_{\text{shatter}}$ (and above $\beta_{\text{unique}}$), metastable states are present in the Gibbs measure and obstruct local Markov chains from mixing rapidly from a worst case initialization. However, it is an open problem whether they still manage to succeed with random initialization.
  4. Above $\beta_{\text{shatter}}$: The Gibbs distribution $\mu$ shatters into exponentially many well-separated clusters, each of which contains only an exponentially small fraction of the Gibbs mass. There is evidence suggesting efficient sampling from $\mu$ is impossible in this region.
Below are some known thresholds for different cases:
  1. SK-Model: It is known that $\beta_{\text{SL}}=\beta_{\text{shatter}}=\beta_{\text{RS}}=1$. OpenAI preprints report that $\beta_{\text{unique}}=1$.

Other results

Parisi formula

The limiting free energy $\lim_{n\to\infty} \tfrac1n \E \log Z_n(\beta)$ exists and equals the infimum of the Parisi functional.

Guerra (upper bound); Talagrand, 2006.

Fast mixing of Glauber at high temperature

For $\beta < 1/4$, Glauber dynamics has spectral gap $\Omega(1/n)$ with high probability over the couplings.

Eldan–Koehler–Zeitouni.
