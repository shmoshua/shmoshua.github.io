Setup

In the spiked Wigner model, we aim to recover an unknown signal $\bs{v}\sim \text{Unif}(\Omega)$, given a noise measurement: $$ M:= W+\frac{\lambda}{N} vv^\top $$ for some signal-to-noise ratio $\lambda\ge 0$, and a noise matrix $W\sim\textsf{GOE}(N)$ with $W_{ji}=W_{ij}\sim \mathcal N(0, 1/N)$ for $i\ne j$, and zero diagonal.

Known Results

Statistical and Spectral Threshold

The spiked Wigner model exhibits a sharp phase transition at $\lambda=1$, known as the BBP transition:

  • If $\lambda<1$, the spiked and unspiked models are mutually contiguous, and weak recovery of the signal is information-theoretically impossible.
  • If $\lambda>1$, the leading eigenvector of $M$ has nontrivial correlation with $\bs{v}$, and weak recovery is achievable in polynomial time.

Glauber Dynamics

For an inverse temperature $\beta>0$, consider the Gibbs measure on $\Omega$: $$ \mu_{\beta M}(x) \propto \exp\left( \frac{\beta}{2}x^\top Mx \right). $$ Equivalently, $$ \mu_{\beta M}(x) \propto \exp\left( \frac{\beta}{2}x^\top Wx + \frac{\beta\lambda}{2N} \langle x,v\rangle^2 \right). $$ The Bayesian posterior corresponds to $\beta=\lambda$. For $\beta\neq\lambda$, this is a tempered version of the posterior.

Polynomial-Time Recovery by Glauber Dynamics

Rajaraman and Wu (2026) show that, under a high-temperature mixing assumption for the SK model, Glauber dynamics exhibits a sharp recovery transition at $\beta\lambda=1.$

More precisely, consider Glauber dynamics initialized at an arbitrary $\bs{\sigma}_0\in\Omega$. Run the chain for $T\geq\widetilde{\Omega}(N^4)$ steps and let $\widehat{\bs{\sigma}}=\bs{\sigma}_t$, where $t$ is chosen uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,T\}$. For $\beta\in(0,1)$ with $\beta\lambda\neq1$, assuming the required modified log-Sobolev inequality (MLSI) for the SK model, the correlation satisfies $$ \mathbb P\left( \left| \frac{1}{N} |\langle\widehat{\bs{\sigma}},\bs{v}\rangle| -\mathsf{OPT}_{\beta,\lambda} \right| >N^{-1/2+\varepsilon} \right)=o_N(1) $$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, where $\mathsf{OPT}_{\beta,\lambda}$ is the largest nonnegative fixed point of an explicit AMP-like recursion. In particular, $$ \mathsf{OPT}_{\beta,\lambda} \begin{cases} =0, & \beta\lambda<1,\\ >0, & \beta\lambda>1. \end{cases} $$ Hence, Glauber dynamics achieves weak recovery when $\beta\lambda>1$, despite potentially having exponential mixing time. Moreover, the correlation attained by Glauber dynamics matches, up to a vanishing error, the correlation of a sample from its stationary Gibbs measure: $$ \frac{1}{N} |\langle\widehat{\bs{\sigma}},\bs{v}\rangle| = \mathsf{OPT}_{\beta,\lambda}+o_N(1). $$ For an $o_N(1)$ approximation without the sharper $N^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$ accuracy, the paper shows that $T=\widetilde{\Omega}(N^3)$ suffices.

Restricted Poincare Inequality

Let us define the following objective set: $$ \mathcal A_{c}:=\{ x\in \Omega : |\langle v, x\rangle | \ge cN\} $$ We define that a reversible Markov Chain $P$ with stationary distribution $\mu$ satisfies the $(\rho,c)$-restricted Poincare inequality, if for any $g:\Omega \to \R$ with $\supp(g)\subseteq \overline{\mathcal A_c}$, $$ \rho \cdot \|g\|^2_\mu\le \dirchform{P}{g}{g} $$ Assume that this holds. Let $\nu_t$ be the law of time $t$ Glauber dynamics starting from $\nu_0$. Further, $f_t := d\nu_t / d\mu$. Let $0 < \delta \le c$. If we have $\varphi:\Omega \to \R$, $$ \varphi(x):=\begin{cases}0 & |\langle v,x\rangle | \ge cN\\ \frac{c - |\langle v, x\rangle | / N}{\delta} & (c-\delta) N \le |\langle v,x\rangle |\le cN\\ 1& |\langle v,x\rangle | < (c-\delta)N\end{cases} $$ Then, $g_t:=\varphi \sqrt{f_t}$ has $\supp(g_t)\subseteq \overline{\mathcal A_{c}}$ and: $$ \|g_t\|^2_\mu=\E_\mu[\varphi^2 f_t]\ge \E_\mu [1_{\overline{\mathcal A_{(c-\delta)}}}f_t]=\nu_t(|\langle v,x\rangle | < (c-\delta)N) $$ For the RHS, Using $(u+w)^2\le(1+\varepsilon)u^2+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})w^2$, $\varphi^2\le1$ and reversibility, $$ \dirchform{P}{g_t}{g_t}\le (1+\varepsilon)\,\dirchform{P}{\sqrt{f_t}}{\sqrt{f_t}}+\frac{2(1+\varepsilon^{-1})}{\delta^2 N}. $$ where a spin flip changes $\langle v,x\rangle$ by $2$, so $|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\le \frac{2}{\delta N}$. So for every $t$, $$ \nu_t\big(|\langle v,x\rangle|<(c-\delta)N\big)\le\frac1\rho\Big[\tfrac{1+\varepsilon}{4}\,\dirchform{P}{f_t}{\log f_t}+\tfrac{2(1+\varepsilon^{-1})}{\delta^2N}\Big]. $$
