--- layout: post title: "Poincare Inequality and MLSI" --- Let $P$ be a reversible Markov chain with a stationary distribution $\mu$.

Linear Algebraic Identities

We have the following identities:
  1. Reversibility implies self-adjointness. Indeed, $$ \langle f,Pg\rangle_\mu = \sum_{x,y}\mu(x)f(x)P(x,y)g(y) =\langle Pf,g\rangle_\mu $$ Therefore, $P$ has real eigenvalues by the spectral theorem.
  2. $P$ has eigenvalues in $[-1,1]$. Indeed, by Jensen $(Pf)^2\le Pf^2$. Therefore, $$ \|Pf\|^2_\mu \le \langle 1,Pf^2\rangle_\mu =\langle 1, f^2\rangle_\mu = \langle f,f\rangle_\mu = \|f\|_\mu^2 $$Therefore, $\|P\|\le 1$. Further, $I-P$ has eigenvalues in $[0,2]$.
  3. Variance as the sum of squares . Let $\{\varphi_i\}_i$ be the eigenbasis of $I-P$ with $\varphi_0=1$. Then, $\E_\mu f = \sum_i \langle f,\varphi_i\rangle_\mu\langle \varphi_i,1\rangle_\mu=\langle f,\varphi_0\rangle_\mu$. Then, by Parseval, $$ \Var_\mu(f)=\E_\mu[(f-\E_\mu f)^2]=\E_\mu\left[\left(\sum_{i\ge 1}\langle f, \varphi_i\rangle_\mu \varphi_i\right)^2\right]=\sum_{i\ge 1}\langle f,\varphi_i\rangle_\mu^2 $$
  4. Dirichlet form as a sum of squares. $$ \dirchform{P}{f}{f}=\langle f,(I-P)f\rangle_\mu = \sum_{i\ge 1}\lambda_i \langle f,\varphi_i\rangle_\mu^2 $$

Poincare Inequality

Definition (Poincare inequality)

$P$ satisfies $\rho$-Poincare inequality if for any function $f:\Omega \to \R$: $$ \rho\cdot \Var_\mu(f)\le \dirchform{P}{f}{f} $$ Then, $\rho_{\text{PI}}$ is the largest such constant $\rho$.

We have that $\rho_{\text{PI}}\ge 1/\poly(n)$ typically.

Let $f_t:=d\nu_t / d\mu$. Then, we have the following results.

  1. The $\chi^2$-distance is the variance of the density. $$ \chi^2(\nu_t\|\mu)=\sum_{x}\frac{(\nu_t(x)-\mu(x))^2}{\mu(x)}= \E_\mu[(f_t -1)^2]=\Var_\mu(f_t) $$
  2. The derivative of the $\chi^2$-distance is the Dirichlet form. $$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\Var_\mu(f_t)&=\frac{d}{dt}\langle f_t -1, f_t-1\rangle_\mu\\ &=\frac{d}{dt}\langle f_t, f_t\rangle_\mu = - 2\langle f_t, (I-P)f_t\rangle = -2\dirchform{P}{f_t}{f_t} \end{aligned} $$
  3. From the Poincare inequality, we have that: $$ \frac{d}{dt} \Var_\mu(f_t)\le -2\rho_{\text{PI}}\cdot \Var_\mu(f_t) $$ By differentiating $g(t):= e^{2\rho_{\text{PI}} t}\Var_\mu(f_t)$, we have that: $$ e^{2\rho_{\text{PI}} t}\Var_\mu(f_t) - \Var_\mu(f_0)=g(t)-g(0)=\int_0^t g'(t)dt \le 0 $$ Therefore, we have that: $$ \begin{aligned} \Var_\mu(f_t)&\le e^{-\rho_{\text{PI}}t} \cdot\Var_\mu(f_0)\\ \chi^2(\nu_t\|\mu)&\le e^{-\rho_{\text{PI}}t} \cdot\chi^2(\nu\|\mu) \end{aligned} $$
  4. $\rho_{\text{PI}}$ is the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of $(I-P)$. Indeed, we have that from above, $$ \lambda_1\cdot \Var_\mu (f)=\lambda_1\cdot \sum_{i\ge 1}\langle f,\varphi_i\rangle ^2\le \sum_{i\ge 1}\lambda_i\langle f,\varphi_i\rangle ^2=\dirchform{P}{f}{f} $$ Hence, $\lambda_1(I-P)\le \rho_{\text{PI}}$. However, we also have that: $$ \rho_{\text{PI}} = \rho_{\text{PI}} \Var_\mu(\varphi_1)\le \dirchform{P}{\varphi_1}{\varphi_1}=\lambda_1 $$

Modified Log-Sobolev Inequality

Definition (MLSI)

$P$ satisfies $\rho$-MLSI if for any function $f:\Omega \to \R_{>0 }$: $$ \rho\cdot \Ent_\mu(f)\le \dirchform{P}{f}{\log f} $$where $\Ent_\mu [f]:=\E_\mu [f\log f] - \E_\mu f \log \E_\mu f$. Then, $\rho_{\text{MLSI}}$ is the largest such constant $\rho$.

Similarly as above, we have the following results.

  1. The KL divergence is the entropy functional of the density. $$ \KL(\nu_t\|\mu)=\sum_{x}\nu_t(x)\log f_t(x)= \langle f_t, \log f_t\rangle_\mu= \Ent_\mu [f_t] $$
  2. The derivative of the KL divergence is the Dirichlet form. $$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\KL(\nu_t\|\mu)&=\frac{d}{dt}\langle f_t, \log f_t\rangle_\mu\\ &= - \langle f_t, (I-P)\log f_t\rangle = -\dirchform{P}{f_t}{\log f_t} \end{aligned} $$
  3. From the MLSI, we have that: $$ \frac{d}{dt} \KL(\nu_t\| \mu)\le -\rho_{\text{MLSI}}\cdot \KL(\nu_t\| \mu) $$ By differentiating $g(t):= e^{\rho_{\text{MLSI}} t}\KL(\nu_t\| \mu)$, we have that: $$ e^{\rho_{\text{MLSI}} t}\KL(\nu_t\| \mu) - \KL(\nu_0\| \mu)=g(t)-g(0)=\int_0^t g'(t)dt \le 0 $$ Therefore, we have that: $$ \begin{aligned} \Ent_\mu(f_t)&\le e^{-\rho_{\text{MLSI}}t} \cdot\Ent_\mu(f_0)\\ \KL(\nu_t\|\mu)&\le e^{-\rho_{\text{MLSI}}t} \cdot\KL(\nu\|\mu) \end{aligned} $$

Weak Poincare Inequality

Definition (WPI)

$P$ satisfies $(\rho,\eps)$-WPI if for any function $f:\Omega \to \R$: $$ \rho\cdot \Var_\mu(f)\le \dirchform{P}{f}{ f} + \eps \|f-\E_\mu f\|^2_\infty $$

Then, using the identities from PI, we have: $$ \chi^2(\nu_t\|\mu)\le e^{-\rho t}\cdot \chi^2(\nu_0\|\mu)+\eps\left\|f_0 - 1\right\|^2_\infty $$

Poincare Constant and MLSI Constant

  1. MLSI implies Poincare $$ \rho_{\text{MLSI}}\le 2\rho_{\text{PI}} $$

    By linearization: Let $g:\Omega \to \R$. We first assume that $\E g = 0$. Let $\eps > 0$ be small enough constant s.t. $f:= 1+\eps g$ is positive. Then, we have: $$ \begin{aligned} \Ent_\mu[f] &=\E_\mu[( 1+\eps g) \log (1+\eps g)] \\ &= \E_\mu\left[\eps g + \frac{\eps^2 g^2}{2}+O(\eps^3)\right]\\ &= \frac{\eps^2}{2} \Var_\mu(g) + O(\eps^3)\\ \dirchform{P}{f}{\log f} &=\eps \langle g, (I-P)\log (1+\eps g)\rangle\\ &=\eps \left\langle g, (I-P)\left(\eps g -\frac{\eps^2 g^2}{2}+O(\eps^3)\right)\right\rangle\\ &= \eps^2\cdot \dirchform{P}{g}{g} + O(\eps^3) \end{aligned} $$ Therefore, by MLSI, $\rho_{\text{MLSI}}\cdot (\eps^2 / 2)\cdot \Var_\mu(g) \le \eps^2\cdot \dirchform{P}{g}{g} + O(\eps^3)$. Diving both sides by $\eps^2$ and taking $\eps\to 0$ gives us our statement.

    ∎

  2. Poincare implies MLSI $$ \rho_{\text{PI}}\le \left(1+\frac{\log(1/\mu_{\min})}{2}\right)\rho_{\text{MLSI}} $$

    Let $f:\Omega \to \R_{>0}$. As both entropy functional and Dirichlet form are linear, we may assume that $\E_\mu f = 1$. Then, $f\le 1/\mu_\min$.

    ∎
