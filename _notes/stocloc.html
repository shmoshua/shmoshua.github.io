--- layout: post title: "Stochastic Localization" --- Let $\Omega:=\{\pm 1\}^n$ be the hypercube. Consider a Gibbs distribution in the Ising model, i.e. $$ \mu_{A,h}(\sigma)\propto \exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^\top A\sigma + \langle h, \sigma\rangle\right) $$ The main idea in stochastic localization is to represent our Gibbs distribution $\mu$ as a random mixture of more concentrated/simpler distributions. Let $\pi=\E_{z\sim\rho}[\pi_z]$ be a measure decomposition. Then, by the law of total variance, for any $f:\Omega \to \R$, $$ \Var_\pi(f) = \E_{z\sim\rho} [\Var_{\pi_z} (f)] +\Var_{z\sim\rho}(\E_{\pi_z}[f]) $$Localization slowly moves all the variance in the first term to the second.

Tilted Measures

Definition (Tilted Measure)

Let $\mu_{A,h}$ be the Gibbs distribution from above. For $\xi\in\R^n$, the tilted measure $\mathcal T_\xi \mu_{A,b}$ is given by: $$ (\mathcal T_\xi \mu_{A,h})(\sigma) \propto \mu_{A,h}(\sigma) \cdot \exp(\langle \xi, \sigma\rangle) $$ i.e. we have that $\mathcal T_\xi \mu_{A,h} = \mu_{A,h+\xi}$.

Let $Z_\xi$ denote the partition function of $\mathcal T_\xi \mu_{A,h}$. Then, we can represent the free energy as a function in $\xi$, i.e. $F(\xi)=-\log Z_\xi$. We have the following identities: $$ \nabla_\xi F = -\E_{\mu_{A,h+\xi}}[\sigma]\in [-1,1]^n,\qquad \nabla^2_\xi F = \Cov_{\mu_{A,h+\xi}}(\sigma) $$ Indeed, we have that $F(\xi) = -\log \sum_{\sigma} \exp(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^\top A\sigma + \langle h+\xi, \sigma\rangle)$. Hence, $$ \nabla_\xi F = - \frac{1}{Z_\xi}\sum_{\sigma}\sigma \exp(\frac{1}{2}\sigma^\top A\sigma + \langle h+\xi, \sigma\rangle) = - \E_{\mu_{A,h+\xi}}[\sigma] $$

Stochastic Localization

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\Omega$. A stochastic localization of $\mu$ is a process $(\mu_t)_{t\ge 0}$ on $\Omega$ s.t.
  1. $\mu_0 = \mu$
  2. (Martingale) For every $\sigma\in \Omega$, the process $t\mapsto \mu_t(\sigma) is a $(\mathcal F_t)$-martingale.
  3. (Localization) Almost surely $\mu_t\to \delta_{\sigma^*}$ for some random $\sigma^*$
