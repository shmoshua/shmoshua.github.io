We work with a binary pairwise factor graph, i.e. a tuple $(G,\varphi,\psi)$ where $G=(V,E)$ is a finite simple graph, each node $v$ admits a unary factor $\varphi_v:\{\pm 1\}\to\R$, and every edge $u\sim v$ admits a binary factor $\psi_{uv}:\{\pm 1\} \to \R$ s.t. $\psi_{uv}(s,t) = \psi_{vu}(t,s)$.

For a given subset $S\subseteq V$, the judge belief is an induced model: $$ \mu_S(x_S)\propto \exp\left(\sum_{uv\in E(G[S])}\psi_{uv}(x_u,x_v) + \sum_{v\in S}\varphi_v(x_v)\right) $$ Then, for $v\in S$, the field of $v$ is given by: $$ h_v:=\frac{1}{2}\log \frac{\mu_S(x_v = 1)}{ \mu_S(x_v = -1)} $$ This is because we have that $\mu_S(x_v )\propto \exp(h_v x_v)$. Consequently, $\E _{\mu_S} x_v = \tanh h_v$.

Unstructured Debate

Assume we have a target statement $t\in V$ that we want to debate on. The debaters have the knowledge of the full model, however, only a smaller induced subgraph is revealed to the judge. The idea is that the debaters cleverly select which nodes to reveal s.t. the judge belief of the target statement is closer to how they want.

More concretely, given $S\subseteq V$, we define the score as the half log-odds: $$ L_S:= \frac{1}{2}\log \left(\frac{\mu_S(x_t = 1)}{1 - \mu_S(x_t = 1)}\right) $$ i.e. $\E_{\mu_S} x_t = \tanh L_S$.

Then, the debate is defined inductively: Let $D_{S,\ell}$ denote the judge decision where the current judge state is $S$ and there are $\ell$ rounds left to the debate. More formally, for every $S\ni t$,

  1. $D_{S,0}:=L_S$
  2. $D_{S,\ell}:= \min_{v\in V \backslash S} D_{S\cup \{v\}, \ell -1}$ where $\ell $ is odd.
  3. $D_{S,\ell}:= \max_{v\in V \backslash S} D_{S\cup \{v\}, \ell -1}$ where $\ell $ is even.
Intuitively, the even rounds want to prove that $t$ is true, and the odd rounds, false. Let further, $D_{k}:= D_{\{t\}, k}$. Then, the goal of debate is to identify, when $D_{2h}\approx L_V$.

Cores and Oscillations

A core is a set $K\ni t$ s.t. $G[K]$ is connected. Then, its oscillation is defined as: $$ \text{osc}(K):=\text{osc}(L|_{\uparrow K}),\qquad \text{where } \uparrow K := \{S\subseteq V : K\subseteq S\} $$
Lemma 1

Let $K$ be a core and $h\ge 0$ s.t. $|K|\le h+1$. Then, $D_{2h}$ is bounded by $L|_{\uparrow K}$, i.e. $$ |D_{2h} - L_V| \le \osc(K) $$

We use this oscillation to bound the gap.

Upper Bound

We can rewrite the binary factors uniquely as follows: $$ \psi_{uv}(s,t)=J_{uv}\,st+a_{u\to v}\,s+a_{v\to u}\,y+\text{const}. $$ Then,

  1. $J_{uv}$ measures how strongly the edge wants $u$ and $v$ to agree or disagree.
  2. $a_{u\to v}$ tries to push $u$ towards true regardless of $v$. This bias appears only once $v$ is also revealed.

Now we can view this as a message-passing procedure. Suppose $G$ is a tree, and cut the edge $e=uv$. Let $T_u,T_v$ denote the two subtrees that contain $u$ and $v$, respectively. Let $h_v$ be the field of $v$ w.r.t. $\mu_{T_v}$, called the cavity field of $v$.

Lemma 2 (One-edge message)

Define the message map of the edge $uv$ towards $u$ as $$ \Phi_{uv}(h):=a_{u\to v}+F_{J_{uv}}\big(h+a_{v\to u}\big),\qquad F_J(y)=\arctanh(\tanh J\tanh y). $$ On a tree, the field of $u$ is its own unary field plus one message per neighbour: $$ H_u=\tfrac12\big(\varphi_u(+)-\varphi_u(-)\big)+\sum_{v\in \partial u}\Phi_{uv}(h_{v\to u}), $$ where $h_{v\to u}$ is the cavity field of $v$ with the edge $uv$ removed.

By definiton of the messages, we have that $m_{v\to u}(s) = \mu_{T_v}(x_v = s) \propto \exp(h_{v\to u} s)$. Then, $$ \begin{aligned} \mu_V(x_u) &\propto \sum_{x_{-u}}\exp\left(\varphi_u(x_u) + \sum_{v\in \partial u}[\psi_{uv}(x_u,x_v)+E_v(x_{T_v})]\right) \\&\propto \exp\left(\varphi_u(x_u)\right)\prod_{v\in \partial u}\sum_{x_{T_v}}\exp\left( \psi_{uv}(x_u,x_v)+E_v(x_{T_v})\right) \\&\propto \exp\left(\varphi_u(x_u)\right)\prod_{v\in \partial u}\sum_{s}\exp\left(\psi_{uv}(x_u,s)\right)\sum_{x_{T_v}: x_v = s} \exp\left(E_v(x_{T_v})\right) \\&\propto \exp\left(\varphi_u(x_u)\right)\prod_{v\in \partial u}\sum_{s}\lambda_v \exp\left(\psi_{uv}(x_u,s) + h_{v\to u} s\right) \\&\propto \exp\left(\varphi_u(x_u)\right)\prod_{v\in \partial u}\lambda_v \sum_{s}\exp\left(J_{uv}x_u s + a_{u\to v} x_u + [a_{v\to u}+ h_{v\to u}] s\right) \\&\propto \exp\left(\varphi_u(x_u)\right)\prod_{v\in \partial u}\lambda_v \exp( a_{u\to v} x_u )\sum_{s}\exp\left(J_{uv}x_u s+ [a_{v\to u}+ h_{v\to u}] s\right) \\&\propto \exp\left(\varphi_u(x_u)\right)\prod_{v\in \partial u}\lambda_v \exp( a_{u\to v} x_u )\cosh\left(J_{uv}x_u + a_{v\to u}+ h_{v\to u}\right) \end{aligned} $$ Therefore, $$ H_u = \frac{1}{2}\log \frac{\mu_V(x_u=1)}{\mu_V(x_u = -1)} = \frac{1}{2}(\varphi_u(+) - \varphi_u(-)) + \sum_{v\in \partial u}\left[a_{u\to v}+\frac{1}{2}\log \left(\frac{\cosh(J_{uv} + a_{v\to u}+ h_{v\to u})}{\cosh(-J_{uv} + a_{v\to u}+ h_{v\to u})}\right)\right] $$

Now, notice that we have: $$ \tanh |F_J(y)| = |\tanh F_J(y)| = |\tanh J| |\tanh y| < |\tanh J|=\tanh |J| $$ As $\tanh$ is increasing, $|F_J(y)|< |J|$.

Let $K$ be a core and $\text{gate}(K)$ be the set of all pairs $(P,e)$ s.t.
  1. $P$ is a directed path in $K$ from $t$ to some $u\in K$.
  2. $e$ is a directed edge from $u$ to some $v\notin K$.
Theorem 2

For arbitrary finite factors, possibly on graphs with cycles, every core $K$ satisfies $$ \text{osc}(K)\;\le\;\sum_{(P,e)\in\text{gate}(K)} I_e\prod_{e'\in P}\tanh\!\big(|J_{e'}|/2\big). $$ In particular, by Lemma 1, if $|K|\le h+1$ then $|D_{2h}-L_V|$ is bounded by the same sum.

Recursive Debate

My question is about finding the threshold for debate, similar to that of uniqueness threshold. Given a graph class, does there exist a threshold under which debate works well, but above which debate provably fails?

In a recursive debate game, we are given a tuple $(G,\varphi,\psi)$. Let $t\in V$ be the target statement. We have Alice and Bob, who have access to the full model. Alice's job is to convince the Judge that the field $h_t$ is some value $a_0$. Then, Bob will recursively have to question this choice, similarly to debate in the classical sense.

Before we discuss the soundness and correctness of the protocol, we introduce the Weisz recursion. AAAA What's the best way to formalize this?

A debate state can be formalized using a tuple $I:=(W,\mathcal E, u)$ where:

  1. $W\subseteq V(G)$ is the set of nodes that are still in the debate. Ironically, its complement are the nodes that were considered already.
  2. $\mathcal E=\{\mathcal E_w \}_{w\in W}$ is the frozen information, where $\mathcal E_w$ is a set of functions $f:\{\pm 1\} \to \R$
  3. $u\in W$ is the new node that is currently under debate.
Then, we are tilting the distribution on $W$ with the frozen information: $$ \nu_I(x_W)\propto \mu_W(x_W)\cdot \exp\left(\sum_{ w\in W}\sum_{f\in \mathcal E_w} f(x_w)\right) $$ We can call this the distribution tilted by $I$. Similarly we define:
  1. titled field at $w$: $h_I(w):= h_{\nu_I}(w)$ for $w\in W$.
  2. tilted local field at $w$: given by: $$ \theta_I(w):= \frac{1}{2}\left[\varphi_w(+1) - \varphi_w(-1)+\sum_{f\in \mathcal E_w}f (+1) - f(-1)\right] $$

Now we look at how a state moves to the next state. Let $I=(W,\mathcal E,u)$ be a state and let $w_1,...,w_m$ be the neighbors of $u$ in $W$. We call them the children of $u$. Then, for each "child" $v$, we can define the new state as: $$ I^{(v)}:=(W':= W \backslash\{u\}, (\mathcal E^{(v)}_w)_{w\in W'}, v) $$ where $$ \mathcal E^{(v)}_w:= \begin{cases}\mathcal E_w\cup\{\psi_{uw}(+1,\cdot)\}&w \text{ is a child and }w < v\\ \mathcal E_w\cup\{\psi_{uw}(-1,\cdot)\}&w \text{ is a child and } v < w\\\mathcal E_w&\text{otherwise}\end{cases} $$

Lemma (Weisz)

Let $I=(W,\mathcal E, u)$ be a debate state. $$ h_{I}(u)=\theta_I(u) + \sum_{v\text{ child}}\Phi_{uv}(h_{I^{(v)}}(v)) $$

Let $w_1\lt\dots\lt w_m$ be the children of $u$ w.r.t. $W$. To compute $h_I(u)$, we first have to find the marginal of $\nu_I$. We have that: $$ \begin{aligned} \nu_I(x_u=s) &\propto\exp\Big(\varphi_u(s)+\sum_{f\in\mathcal E_u}f(s)\Big)\sum_{y}\Omega(y)\exp\Big(\sum_{i=1}^m\psi_{uw_i}(s,y_{w_i})\Big)\\ &=\exp\Big(\varphi_u(s)+\sum_{f\in\mathcal E_u}f(s)\Big)\pi(s,...,s) \end{aligned} $$ where $$ \Omega(y):=\exp\Big(\sum_{vw\in E(G[W'])}\psi_{vw}(y_v,y_w)+\sum_{v\in W'}\varphi_v(y_v)+\sum_{w\in W'}\sum_{f\in\mathcal E_w}f(y_w)\Big) $$ collects every factor not involving $x_u$, and $$ \pi: \{\pm 1\}^m \to \R,\quad \sigma\mapsto \sum_{y}\Omega(y)\exp\Big(\sum_{i=1}^m\psi_{uw_i}(\sigma_i,y_{w_i})\Big). $$

Hence, we get that: $$ h_I(u) = \theta_{I}(u) + \frac{1}{2}\log \frac{\pi(1,...,1)}{\pi(-1,...,-1)} $$

Now, let $\sigma^{(k)}:=1^k(-1)^{m-k}$. Then, we notice that: $$ \frac{\pi(1,...,1)}{\pi(-1,...,-1)} = \frac{\pi(\sigma^{(m)})}{\pi(\sigma^{(0)})} = \prod_{k=1}^m \frac{\pi(\sigma^{(k)})}{\pi(\sigma^{(k-1)})} $$ Then, $$ \frac{\pi(\sigma^{(k)})}{\pi(\sigma^{(k-1)})} = \frac{\sum_{t}\nu_{I^{(w_k)}}(x_{w_k}=t)\exp\Big(\psi_{uw_k}(+1,t)\Big)}{\sum_{t}\nu_{I^{(w_k)}}(x_{w_k}=t)\exp\Big(\psi_{uw_k}(-1,t)\Big)} =\frac{\sum _t \exp( h_{I^{(w_k)}}(w_k)t + \psi_{uw_k}(+1,t))}{\sum_t\exp( h_{I^{(w_k)}}(w_k)t + \psi_{uw_k}(-1,t))} $$ By using that: $ \sum_t e^{ h_{I^{(w_k)}}(w_k)t + \psi_{uw_k}(s,t)}\propto e^{a_{u\to w_k}s}\cdot\cosh(J_{uw_k}s+h_{I^{(w_k)}}(w_k)+a_{w_k\to u})$ over $s$, we get that: $$ \frac{\pi(\sigma^{(k)})}{\pi(\sigma^{(k-1)})} = e^{2a_{u\to w_k}}\frac{\cosh(J_{uw_k}+h_{I^{(w_k)}}(w_k)+a_{w_k\to u})}{\cosh(-J_{uw_k}+h_{I^{(w_k)}}(w_k)+a_{w_k\to u})} =2 \Phi_{uw_k}(h_{I^{(w_k)}}(w_k)) $$ This proves the statement.

Recursive Debate on Loopy Graphs

Now, we introduce the following debate protocol.
Definition

Given a setting $(G,\varphi,\psi)$, a target statement $t\in V(G)$, and a error margin $\eps$,

  1. Alice announces $a_0\in \R$. The initial state of the debate is $I_0:= (V,\varnothing, t)$.
  2. For rounds $r = 0,...,R-1$, with the current state $I_r =(W_r, \mathcal E_r, u_r)$ and the announcement $a_r$:
    1. The Judge calls an oracle that returns $\varphi_{u_r}$ and $(v,\psi_{u_rv})_{v\in \partial u_r}$.
    2. The Judge computes the tilted local field $\theta_{I_r}(u_r)$.
    3. If $u_r$ has no children, Alice wins if and only if $|a_r - \theta_{I_r}(u_r)|\le \eps$.
    4. Otherwise, for each child $w$ of $u_r$, Alice announces the tilted field $h_w$ of $w$. Alice loses, if: $$ \left|a_r - \theta_r (u_r)-\sum_{w \in \partial u_r \backslash U_r}\Phi_{u_r w}(h_w)\right|> \eps $$
    5. Bob selects a child $w$ and $I_{r+1}:= I^{(w)}_r$ and $a_{r+1}:= h_w$.
  3. For round $R$, Alice wins if and only if $|a_R - \theta_{I_R}(u_R)| \le \eps$.

For the given debate setting, let $\mathcal A(R,\eps)\subseteq \R$ be the set of $a_0$ for which Alice has a strategy to win against every strategy of Bob. It is not difficult to see that the debate protocol is complete. But for completeness sake (haha)

Let $I:=(W,\mathcal E, u)$ be a debate state. Then, we set $h^{(k)}_I(u)$ to be the tilted field after $k$ steps, i.e. inductively, $$ h^{(0)}_I(u) := \theta_I(u),\qquad h^{(k)}_I(u) := \theta_I(u) + \sum_{v\text{ child}}\Phi_{uv}(h^{(k-1)}_{I^{(v)}}(v)) $$

Let $I_0:= (V,\varnothing, t)$. Then, we claim that $h^{(R)}_{I_0}(t) \in \mathcal A(R,\eps)$. This is given by Alice announcing $h_v= h^{(R - r - 1)}_{I^{(v)}_{r}}(v)$ at every step.

For soundness, we introduce the following class of debate settings: Let $\mathcal G_\text{RD}(d,a_{\max}, J_{\max})$ be the set of binary factor graphs s.t. for all $(G,\varphi,\psi)\in \mathcal G_\text{RD}(d,a_{\max}, J_{\max})$:

  1. $d$ is the max degree,
  2. $|a_{u\to v}|\le a_{\max}$ for all $u,v$,
  3. $|J_{uv}|\le J_{\max}$ for all $u,v$.
Then, we can show the soundness of the protocol on this family as follows.

Theorem (Soundness)

Let $(G,\varphi,\psi)\in\mathcal G_\text{RD}(d,a_{\max}, J_{\max})$. For any $R\ge 1$, $\eps \ge 0$, and $a_0\in \mathcal A(R,\eps)$: $$ |a_0 - h^{(R)}_{I_0}(t)|\le \eps \left(1 + d\tanh J_{\max}\cdot \sum_{j=0}^{R-1}\lambda^j\right) $$ where $\lambda := (d-1)\tanh J_{\max}$.

The proof follows an induction. Let $I_r$ and $a_r$ be given from a debate protocol where Alice wins. We show via induction on $R-r$ that if $|a_r - h^{(R-r)}_{I_r}(u_r)|> \eps\sum_{j=0}^{R-r}\lambda^j$, then Bob has a winning strategy from $I_r$.

At round $r=R$ or if $u_r$ has no child, we have that $h_{I_r}^{R-r}(u_r) = \theta_{I_r}(u_r)$ and as $\sum_{j=0}^{R-r}\lambda^j\ge 1$, Alice loses. Otherwise, assume that the check in (25) passes. We have that: $$ \left|\sum_{v\text{ child}}\Phi_{u_r v}(h_v) -\sum_{v\text{ child}}\Phi_{u_r v}(h^{(R-(r+1))}_{I_r^{(v)}}(v)) \right|\ge |a_r - h^{(R-r)}_{I_r}(u_r)| - \eps > \eps\sum_{j=1}^{R-r}\lambda ^j $$ Then, by triangle inequality, there exists a child $v$ s.t. $$ \varepsilon\cdot \tanh J_{\max} \cdot \sum_{j = 0}^{R-(r+1)} \lambda^j < \left|\Phi_{u_rv} (h_v) - \Phi_{u_rv}(h^{(R-(r+1))}_{I_r^{(v)}}(v))\right| $$ Using that $\Phi_{u_r v}$ is $\tanh J_{\max}$-Lipschitz, Bob can choose this child and by induction hypothesis Bob has a winning strategy.

For the theorem statement, it is analogous except that instead of $d-1$ children $t$ has at most $d$ children.

Corollary

Let $\rho:= (d-1)\tanh J_{\max} < 1$. For every instance of $\mathcal G_\text{RD}(d,a_{\max}, J_{\max})$, every target $t$, any $R\ge 1$, $\eps \ge 0$, and $a_0\in \mathcal A(R,\eps)$: $$ |a_0 - h_{\mu_V}(t) | \le d (a_{\max} + J_{\max})\rho^R+ \eps\left( 1+ \frac{d}{d-1}\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right) $$

It suffices to show that $|h^{(R)}_{I_0}(t) - h_{\mu_V}(t)|\le d(a_\max + J_\max) \rho^R$. Let $\Delta_r$ be the supremum of $|h^{(R-r)}_{I_r}(u_r) - h_{I_r}(u_r)|$ over all possible $I_r$.

At $r=R$, for any $I_R$, we have that: $$ |h^{(0)}_{I_0}(t) - h_{\mu_V}(t)| = \left|\sum_{v\text{ child}}\Phi_{_{u_R v}}(h_{I_r^{(v)}}(v))\right|\le (d-1) (a_{\max}+J_\max) $$ For $r< R$, we have that: $$ \begin{aligned} |h^{(R-r)}_{I_r}(u_r) - h_{I_r}(u_r)| &\le \sum_{v\text{ child}}\left|\Phi_{u_rv}(h^{(R-(r+1))}_{I_r^{(v)}}(v)) -\Phi_{u_r v}(h_{I_r^{(v)}}(v))\right|\\ &\le \rho\Delta_{r+1} \end{aligned} $$ As we have $d$ instead of $d-1$ at only at the root, we have that $\Delta_0 \le d(a_\max + J_\max)\rho^R$.

Comparison with the tree uniqueness threshold

Here we will relate to our findings on debate to a well-known identity in statistical physics. The tree uniqueness threshold is a condition for the strength of the interaction and degree of a tree s.t. the spins on the nodes far away from the root cannot influence the field on the root. More formally,

Definition (Tree uniqueness)

Let $T_d$ be the infinite rooted tree with root $t$ in which every vertex has $d$ children, $T_d^L$ its truncation at depth $L$, and $\partial_L$ the vertices at depth $L$. For $J\in\R$ and pinning $\tau\in\{\pm1\}^{\partial_L}$, let $$ \mu^\tau_L(x)\propto\exp\left(\sum_{v\sim w}Jx_vx_w\right)\mathbf 1\{x_{\partial_L}=\tau\} $$ on $T_d^L$. The model has uniqueness on $T_d$ if $$ \lim_{L\to\infty}\sup_{\tau,\tau'}\big|\mu^\tau_L(x_t=+1)-\mu^{\tau'}_L(x_t=+1)\big|=0 . $$ This holds if and only if $d\tanh|J|\le1$.
