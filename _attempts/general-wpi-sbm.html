--- title: "Generalized Weak Poincaré for SBM Weak Recovery" status: open summary: "Weak recovery from Glauber via a windowed flow-LP certificate at a temperature hotter than Bayes. The reduction is proved; one dynamical lemma is open." ---

Weak Poincare inequality

Let $\mu$ be the posterior. For weak recovery, we denote the magnetization as $m(\sigma):= \langle x^*,\sigma\rangle / n$ where $x^*$ is the truth. So, we want a weak Poincare inequality of the following form: $$ \E_\mu g^2 \le \rho \dirchform{P}{g}{g} + \eps \E_\mu [m^2 g^2] $$ for all even $g$. Goal is to find whether we can have $\rho =n^{O(1)}$ and small $\eps$. If this holds, then for any even law $\nu$ with $f:= d\nu / d\mu$, by taking $g:= \sqrt{f}$ we have: $$ 1 = \E_\nu[1] =\E_\mu[g^2] \le \frac{\rho}{4} \dirchform{P}{f}{\log f}+\eps \E_\nu[m^2] $$ Therefore, $$ \E_{\nu}[m^2]\ge \frac{1-\frac{\rho}{4} \dirchform{P}{f}{\log f}}{\eps} $$

This might be too strong. If there is a single trap, this might fail?

Find the dual?

Let us define: $$ \kappa(u):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c} \E_\mu g^2 = 1 \\ \E_\mu m^2 g^2 \le u\end{subarray}} \dirchform{P}{g}{g} $$ If $\kappa(u)\ge \kappa_0> 0$, then for any $g$ with $\E_\mu g^2 =1$, we have $\kappa_0\le \dirchform{P}{g}{g}$ or $u\le \E_\mu m^2 g^2 $. Hence, $$ \E_\mu g^2 \le \frac{1}{\kappa_0}\dirchform{P}{g}{g} + \frac{1}{u} \E_\mu m^2 g^2 $$ This can be generalized to any $g$ by taking $g / \|g\|_\mu$.

Now the dual is given by, finding a positive $h$ s.t. $$ \frac{(I-P)h}{h}+\theta m^2 \ge \kappa $$, then we have that: $$ \E_\mu g^2 \le \frac{1}{\kappa}\dirchform{P}{g}{g} +\frac{\theta }{\kappa} \E_\mu m^2 g^2 $$

WPI with some test functions

Two test functions show what the WPI demands.

  1. $g\equiv 1$ gives $\eps\ge 1/\E_\mu m^2$. So "small $\eps$" can only mean $\eps=O(1)$.
  2. $g=\mathbf 1_S$ gives, for every set $S$ with conductance $\Phi(S)$, $$ 1\le \rho\,\Phi(S)+\eps\,\E_\mu[m^2\mid S]. $$ Every low-conductance set must carry overlap, whatever its mass. Up to polynomial losses, the WPI is equivalent to recovery from every starting state.

Evenness changes nothing: $(I-P)+\theta m^2$ commutes with the global flip, so its ground state, which is the optimal $h$, is already even. A canonical explicit dual is the landscape function $h=\big((I-P)+\theta m^2\big)^{-1}\mathbf 1$, with $\kappa=1/\|h\|_\infty$. It is the expected lifetime of the chain killed at rate $\theta m^2$. On exact small instances ($n\approx 12$) it is within $10\%$ of the optimum.

Failure: there is no closed form for $h$ beyond Curie–Weiss. There $h=\mu_m^{-1/2}$ works because $m$ evolves autonomously. For the SBM the drift of $m$ is dominated by other components, so a potential in $m$ alone is not a valid certificate.

Are there traps?

Two-replica (Franz–Parisi) cavity at replica-symmetric level, plus the $x=1$ reconstruction test:

  • the constrained free entropy $s(m)$ increases monotonically up to the equilibrium overlap;
  • every overlap slice is a single state (replicon $\lt 1$, no reconstruction).

Simulations at $n$ up to $80$k: no start ever gets stuck. This includes $m\approx 0$ states pinned and annealed at the Bayes temperature and colder, intermediate-overlap pins, and wrong-sign domains.

Deep traps are invisible from the uniform start. By $L^p$ contraction of the flux into $B$, the chain enters $B$ only if $\Phi(B)\gtrsim e^{-(s(0)-\log 2)n}$, which is $\approx e^{-0.44n}$ at $\lambda=1.5$.

What remains: rare, frozen states (1RSB with Parisi parameter $\lt 1$) are not ruled out. The WPI needs them excluded; the uniform start does not, if they are deep.

Restricting function space further

We only need the weak Poincare to hold for $\nu_t$ where $t\sim \text{Unif}[0,T]$. This means we can have following constraints:

  1. $\nu$ is even.
  2. We have that: $$ 0\le f_T = f_0 -\int_0^T (I-P) f_\tau d\tau = f_0 -T(I-P) \bar f,\qquad \bar f=\frac1T\int_0^T f_\tau d\tau . $$
Hence, we can solve the optimization problem: $$ \begin{array}{rl} \inf& \E_\mu [m^2 f]\\ \text{s.t.}& f\ge 0\\ & \E_\mu f = 1 \\ & \Ent_\mu[f]\le \text{KL}(\nu_0\|\mu)\\ & \dirchform{P}{f}{\log f} \le \text{KL}(\nu_0\|\mu) / T\\ & (I-P) f \le f_0/T \end{array} $$

(The entropy constraint holds by convexity and monotonicity of KL. The Dirichlet constraint holds because $(a,b)\mapsto(a-b)(\log a-\log b)$ is jointly convex and $\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{KL}(\nu_t\|\mu)=-\dirchform{P}{f_t}{\log f_t}$.)

Only the last constraint matters: the flow LP

In measure form, with generator $L$: $$ \inf\ \nu(m^2)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \nu\ge0,\ \ \nu(\mathbf 1)=1,\ \ \nu_0+T\,\nu L\ge 0 . $$ Lemma 5 (weak duality). If $f\ge0$ and $Lf\le m^2-c$ everywhere, then every feasible $\nu$ has $\nu(m^2)\ge c-\nu_0(f)/T$. In particular $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c-\E_{\nu_0}f/T$. Proof. $(\nu_0+T\nu L)(f)\ge0$, since both factors are nonnegative. So $T\nu(Lf)\ge-\nu_0(f)$, and $\nu(m^2)-c\ge\nu(Lf)\ge-\nu_0(f)/T$. $\square$ Probabilistically this is Dynkin's formula, $\E f(X_T)-\E f(X_0)=\E\int_0^TLf$, together with $f\ge0$.

Lemma 6 (WPI $\Rightarrow$ flow-LP dual). If $-Lh/h+\theta m^2\ge\kappa$, then $f=\frac1\theta(\log h-\min\log h)$ satisfies $Lf\le m^2-\kappa/\theta$. Proof. $\log(h(y)/h(x))\le h(y)/h(x)-1$ gives $L\log h\le Lh/h\le\theta m^2-\kappa$. $\square$

Lemma 7 (flow-LP dual $\Rightarrow$ WPI, polynomial loss). If $0\le f\le F$ and $Lf\le m^2-c$, set $\theta=\min\{F^{-1},c/(enF^2)\}$ and $h=e^{\theta f}$. Then $-Lh/h+\theta m^2\ge\theta c/2$, i.e. $\rho=O(nF^2)$ and $\eps=2/c$. Proof. $|\theta\Delta_if|\le1$ and $e^a-1-a\le\frac e2a^2$ for $|a|\le1$ give $Lh/h\le\theta Lf+\frac e2\theta^2\sum_ir_i(\Delta_if)^2\le\theta(m^2-c)+\frac e2\theta^2nF^2\le\theta(m^2-c)+\theta c/2$. $\square$

So with all starting states the two are equivalent up to polynomial factors. The difference is the start cost: $\E_{\nu_0}f$ for the flow LP, $\text{KL}(\nu_0\|\mu)/(4\theta)$ ($\approx0.2n$ here) for the WPI.

Failure: by Rost's filling scheme, the feasible $\nu$ are exactly the occupation measures of randomized stopping times with mean $T$. An adversary stops recovering paths early and keeps rare trapped ones, so a trap entered with probability $p$ and residence $R$ breaks the LP once $pR\gtrsim T$. Toy chain ($p=1\%$, $R=10^7$): truth $0.99$, LP $0$, and the entropy and Dirichlet constraints do not help. Exact $n=10$ SBM posterior (no traps): LP $0.72$ vs truth $0.73$.

Exceptional sets

Lemma 8 (stopped dual). If $0\le f\le F$ and $Lf\le m^2-c$ on $D$, then $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c\,\mathbb P(\tau_{D^c}\gt T)-F/T$. Proof. Dynkin's formula up to $\tau=T\wedge\tau_{D^c}$ (where $X_r\in D$ for $r\lt\tau$) gives $c\,\E\tau\le\E\int_0^\tau m^2+\E f(X_0)$, and $\E\tau\ge T\,\mathbb P(\tau_{D^c}\gt T)$. $\square$

Lemma 9 (occupation defect). If $Lf\le m^2-c+e$ everywhere with $e\ge0$, then $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c-F/T-\frac1T\int_0^T\E\,e(X_t)\,dt$. Proof. The same Dynkin argument. $\square$

Failure: both need a trajectory statement (the cold start rarely enters, or rarely occupies, the bad set). At the Bayes temperature the hard states are not separable from cold-start states by a simple static statistic: in the box $\{|m|\le\delta,\ \hat q\ge q_0\}$ the margin was $0.751$ (cold) against $0.755$ (pinned).

Changing the temperature

Run Glauber for $\mu_\beta\propto e^{\beta\sum J_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j}$ with the Bayes couplings $J_e=\operatorname{atanh}\omega$ on edges and $J_n\approx-\frac{a-b}{2n}$ on non-edges. Here $\omega=\frac{a-b}{a+b}$, $\lambda=d\omega^2$, $t=\tanh(\beta J_e)$, $x=dt^2$.

Heuristic (cavity). The equilibrium is informative iff $d\omega t\gt1$ ($x\gt1/\lambda$) and the noise is non-glassy iff $x\lt1$. At the Bayes temperature $x=\lambda\gt1$, and the $m=0$ slice is locally ordered (self-overlap $0.21$ at $\lambda=1.5$, $0.36$ at $\lambda=2$): those are the pinned states that no explicit $h$ certified. In the window $x\in(1/\lambda,1)$ the $m=0$ slice is the paramagnet. At the balanced point $\sqrt{\lambda x}-1=1-x$, i.e. $$ x_\star=\tfrac12\Big((4+\lambda)-\sqrt{(4+\lambda)^2-16}\Big),\qquad \text{margins } 0.37,\,0.24,\,0.14,\,0.09\ \text{ for }\lambda=3,2,1.5,1.3 . $$ Liu–Mohanty–Rajaraman–Wu prove mixing only for $x\le0.03$, which forces $\lambda\gtrsim1/0.03$.

Proposition 10 (spectral form of the window; sketch). Put $r=1/t$. Then $H(t)=I+\frac{t^2}{1-t^2}D-\frac{t}{1-t^2}A\ \propto\ (r^2-1)I+D-rA$, the Bethe Hessian at $r$. By Ihara–Bass, $\det(I-uB)=(1-u^2)^{|E|-n}\det\big(I-uA+u^2(D-I)\big)$ for the non-backtracking matrix $B$. With $u=1/r$, zeros of $\det H(r)$ for $r\gt1$ occur exactly at real eigenvalues of $B$. As $r\to\infty$, $H(r)\succ0$, so the number of negative eigenvalues equals the number of real $B$-eigenvalues above $r$. For the SBM with $\lambda\gt1$ these are, w.h.p., $\approx d$ (trivial) and $\approx d\omega$ (informative), with the bulk in radius $\approx\sqrt d$ (Bordenave–Lelarge–Massoulié). Hence $$ \text{window}\iff\sqrt d\lt\tfrac1t\lt d\omega\iff H(t)\text{ has exactly two negative directions (uniform, informative).} $$ The uniform one is neutralized by $J_n$: its net linear coefficient is $\approx dt-\beta d\omega$, which is $0.07$–$0.38$ in our settings, all $\lt1$.

Claim 11 (no uniform gap on the bulk). There is no $n$-independent $\delta\gt0$ with $H(t)\succeq\delta I$ on the complement of those two directions. Proof. On an isolated $k$-star, let $a$ be $r$ at the center and $1$ at the leaves. Then $a^\top((r^2-1)I+D-rA)a=(r^2-1)(r^2+k)+kr^2+k-2kr^2=(r^2-1)r^2$ and $\|a\|^2=k+r^2$, so the Rayleigh quotient of $H(t)$ is $r^2/(k+r^2)$. For every fixed $k$ such stars have positive density, so three disjoint ones give a vector orthogonal to any two directions with quotient $\to0$ as $k\to\infty$. $\square$ (Small components cannot be recovered anyway; a correct statement must restrict to a trimmed core.)

Windowed certificate

Coordinate $y=\langle\xi,\sigma\rangle/n$, where $\xi$ is the informative non-backtracking (or Bethe-Hessian) vector with $\|\xi\|^2=n$. Potential $\psi=\ell(y^2)$, with $\ell(q)=\log\frac{z^2+\eta}{q+\eta}$ for $q\le z^2$ continued smoothly to $0$, and $\eta=c_\eta/n$, so $\psi_{\max}=\log(1+z^2n/c_\eta)=\log n+O(1)$.

The theorem

Theorem 12. Let $\psi\in[0,\psi_{\max}]$, $\alpha\ge0$, $0\le s_1\lt s_2$, $\ell=s_2-s_1$, $c\in(0,1]$, $D\subseteq\Omega$, and suppose for all $x\in D$ $$ \E_x\Big[\frac1\ell\int_{s_1}^{s_2}m(X_r)^2dr\Big]+\frac\alpha\ell\,\E_x\big[\psi(X_{s_1})-\psi(X_{s_2})\big]\ \ge\ c .\tag{$\star$} $$ Then for every initial law $\nu$ and $T\gt0$: $\E_\nu m(X_U)^2\ge c\,\mathbb P_\nu(\tau_{D^c}\gt T)-\big(\alpha\psi_{\max}+\tfrac{s_1+s_2}{2}\big)/T$.

Proof. Let $K=\frac1\ell\int_{s_1}^{s_2}P_rdr$, $\omega(r)=1$ on $[0,s_1]$ and $\omega(r)=(s_2-r)/\ell$ on $[s_1,s_2]$, and $$ f=\alpha K\psi+\int_0^{s_2}\omega(r)P_r(1-m^2)\,dr . $$ Then $0\le f\le\alpha\psi_{\max}+\int_0^{s_2}\omega=\alpha\psi_{\max}+\frac{s_1+s_2}{2}$. Using $LP_r=\frac{d}{dr}P_r$: $LK\psi=\frac1\ell(P_{s_2}\psi-P_{s_1}\psi)$, and integrating by parts, $L\int_0^{s_2}\omega P_r\phi\,dr=[\omega P_r\phi]_0^{s_2}-\int\omega'P_r\phi\,dr=-\phi+K\phi$. With $\phi=1-m^2$, $$ Lf=\frac\alpha\ell\big(P_{s_2}\psi-P_{s_1}\psi\big)+m^2-Km^2 , $$ so $(\star)$ is exactly $Lf\le m^2-c$ on $D$. Lemma 8 finishes. $\square$

Corollary 13. If $\mathbb P(\tau_{D^c}\le T)\le\frac14$ and $T\ge4(\alpha\psi_{\max}+\frac{s_1+s_2}{2})/c$, then $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c/2$ and $\mathbb P(|m(X_U)|\ge\sqrt{c/4})\ge c/4$. Proof. $c(1-\delta)-c/4\ge c/2$. For $w=m^2\in[0,1]$, $\E w\le\mathbb P(w\ge\E w/2)+\E w/2$. $\square$ With $\psi_{\max}\approx\log n$ this is $O(\log n)$ sweeps, i.e. $O(n\log n)$ updates.

The window does not give a stronger bound than the flow LP; it is a way to build a good dual point from a simple $\psi$. As $s_2\downarrow s_1=s$, $(\star)$ becomes $\E_x[m(X_s)^2-\alpha L\psi(X_s)]\ge c$ (the lookahead form), and $s=0$ is plain dual feasibility of $\alpha\psi$.

Properties of the potential

Lemma 14 (second-order expansion). Let $\delta_i=-2\xi_i\sigma_i/n$, $r_i=\frac12(1-\sigma_i\tanh\beta h_i)$, $$ b:=Ly=\frac1n\sum_i\xi_i(\tanh\beta h_i-\sigma_i),\qquad q:=\sum_ir_i\delta_i^2=\frac{2}{n^2}\sum_i\xi_i^2(1-\sigma_i\tanh\beta h_i). $$ Then $L\psi=\psi'(y)b+\frac12\psi''(y)q+\varrho$ with $|\varrho|\le\frac43\|\psi'''\|_\infty\|\xi\|_\infty/n^2$. Proof. $r_i\delta_i=\frac{\xi_i}{n}(\tanh\beta h_i-\sigma_i)$ gives the first-order term. Taylor leaves $\frac16\|\psi'''\|_\infty\sum_ir_i|\delta_i|^3\le\frac16\|\psi'''\|_\infty\frac{8}{n^3}\|\xi\|_\infty\sum_i\xi_i^2$. $\square$

For the log potential $\|\psi'''\|_\infty\lesssim\eta^{-3/2}$, so $\varrho=O(\|\xi\|_\infty c_\eta^{-3/2}n^{-1/2})$. In scaled variables $Z=\sqrt n\,y$, $B=\sqrt n\,b$, $\bar q=\frac n2q$, for $|y|\lt z$: $$ -L\psi=\frac{2ZB}{Z^2+c_\eta}+\frac{2\bar q\,(c_\eta-Z^2)}{(Z^2+c_\eta)^2}+o(1).\tag{$\diamond$} $$ The first term is drift, good when $|y|$ grows. The second is diffusion: good inside the flat region, bad outside it.

Lemma 15 (uniform diffusion floor). For every $\sigma$, $\bar q(\sigma)\ge\frac1n\sum_i\xi_i^2e^{-2\beta(J_e\deg_i+|J_n|n)}$. Proof. $1-\sigma\tanh\beta h\ge1-\tanh\beta|h|=\frac{2}{1+e^{2\beta|h|}}\ge e^{-2\beta|h|}$ and $|h_i|\le J_e\deg_i+|J_n|(n-1)$. $\square$ Measured: $\bar q\in[0.23,0.43]$ on every configuration examined.

Lemma 16 (size of the drift under linear growth). If $B=\gamma Z$ and $\bar q_-\le\bar q\le\bar q_+$, take $c_\eta=2\bar q_+/\gamma$ and $\rho_q=\bar q_-/\bar q_+$. Then $(\diamond)\ge\frac78\rho_q\gamma$. Proof. Put $\varrho=Z^2/c_\eta$ (this is not the remainder from Lemma 14). For $\varrho\le1$ the diffusion term is $\ge\gamma\rho_q\frac{1-\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^2}$, so $(\diamond)/\gamma\ge\frac{2\varrho}{1+\varrho}+\rho_q\frac{1-\varrho}{(1+\varrho)^2}\ge\rho_q\frac{2\varrho^2+\varrho+1}{(1+\varrho)^2}$. For $\varrho\ge1$, use $\bar q\le\bar q_+$ in the negative term: $(\diamond)/\gamma\ge\frac{2\varrho^2+\varrho+1}{(1+\varrho)^2}\ge1$. Finally $\frac{d}{d\varrho}\frac{2\varrho^2+\varrho+1}{(1+\varrho)^2}=\frac{3\varrho-1}{(1+\varrho)^3}$, so the minimum is $\frac78$ at $\varrho=\frac13$. $\square$ Then Corollary 13 needs $T\approx\frac{32}{7}\frac{\log n}{\gamma}$, the observed escape scale. Empirically $\gamma\approx0.4\,|\lambda_{\min}(H(t))|$.

How we got to the window (each step with $D=\Omega$)

  1. $f=\alpha\psi$. Fails: at many configurations the drift of $y$ momentarily points the wrong way.
  2. $f=\alpha P_s\psi+\int_0^sP_r(1-m^2)dr$, for which $Lf=P_s(\alpha L\psi-m^2)+m^2$. Works for $\lambda\ge2$; inconclusive for $\lambda\le1.5$, because the replica noise is as large as the signal $\approx2\gamma$.
  3. $s_1=0$ (integrated). Fails: averages over the initial transient, during which $|y|$ shrinks.
  4. Burn-in plus window. Works at $\lambda=3,2,1.5,1.3$.

What is left

Main Lemma A (conjecture, $D=\Omega$). W.h.p. over $G$ there are $\alpha,c,s_1,s_2,c_\eta,z\gt0$ independent of $n$ such that $(\star)$ holds at every configuration. Then, by Theorem 12, recovery holds from any start in $O(n\log n)$ updates, and by Lemma 7 there is a WPI with $\rho=O(n\log^2n)$ and $\eps=2/c$.

Why it is hard: a local proof would need contraction under worst-case boundary conditions, roughly $dt\lt1$. Informative growth needs $d\omega t\gt1$, and $\omega\lt1$ forces $dt\gt1$. So a global ingredient is needed (Claim 11 rules out the naive spectral one). Configurations with $|y|\ge z$ and $m\approx0$, where $\psi$ is flat, were never sampled.

Main Lemma T (conjecture, cold start). Along the uniform-start trajectory, $(\star)$ holds up to a defect $e$ with $\frac1T\int_0^T\E\,e(X_t)dt\le c/4$. Then Lemma 9 gives $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c/2$.

Why it is hard: it needs control at the $n^{-1/2}$ scale over $\log n$ sweeps, including the global magnetization term $J_n\sum_j\sigma_j$. Glauber on a tree is a dynamic-cavity problem over trajectories, not a scalar recursion.

Update: a correlation route for the cold start

The tree computation, and why short times are not enough

On the Poisson$(d)$ tree with edge coupling $\operatorname{atanh}\omega_\beta$ and labels broadcast with edge correlation $\omega$, let $\chi(s)=\partial_h\E_h[x^*_o\sigma_o(s)]\big|_{h=0}$ be the root's response to the seeded product start $\mathbb P_h(\sigma_v(0)=\pm1)=\frac{1\pm h x^*_v}{2}$. Differentiating the initial law gives $$ \chi(s)=\E\sum_v\omega^{\operatorname{dist}(o,v)}\,\E_0[\sigma_o(s)\sigma_v(0)],\qquad \chi(0)=1 . $$ The exact formulas are $\chi'(0)=\omega S-1$ and $\chi''(0)=1-2\omega S+S\,T_\omega$, where $S=\E[D\,a_D]$, $T_\omega=\E[a_{1+L'}(1+L'\omega^2)]$, $D,L'\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(d)$, and $a_k=\E\big[\varsigma_1\tanh(\beta J_e\textstyle\sum_{j\le k}\varsigma_j)\big]$ for independent uniform signs $\varsigma_j$. With Taylor remainders uniform in the depth, they give a strict gain $\chi(2s)\gt\chi(s)$ at $s=10^{-6}$ near the line $\omega S=1$. I checked the derivations and the numbers.

This does not reach our regime. The gain is just "$\chi'(0)\approx0$, $\chi''(0)\gt0$". On the balanced curve the line $\omega S=1$ sits at $\lambda\approx3.41$. For $\lambda=3,2,1.5,1.3$ the initial slope is $-0.04,-0.18,-0.27,-0.31$. On the SBM graphs the measured $\chi$ dips by 25–30% at $\lambda=1.5$ and $1.3$. Its minimum is at about 3–5 sweeps for $\lambda=1.5$; at $\lambda=1.3$ it stays flat until about 20 sweeps. Both are far outside the Taylor radius $s\le 1/5$. The sign of $\chi'(0)$ is also not the informative condition: at $\omega_\beta=0.4$, a zero slope needs $d\omega\omega_\beta\approx1.53$, not $1$. What matters is the long-time growth of $\chi$.

Lemma 17 (correlation reduction)

Start from the uniform law $\nu_0$ on the finite graph, including the non-edge term. For every $s\ge0$ and every statistic $P$ of the path up to time $s$ with $\E P^2\gt0$, $$ \E\,m(X_s)^2\ \ge\ \frac{\E[m(X_s)P]^2}{\E[P^2]} . $$ With $P=m(X_0)$, so that $\E\,m(X_0)^2=1/n$, $$ \E\,m(X_s)^2\ \ge\ \frac{\chi_n(s)^2}{n},\qquad \chi_n(s)=\tfrac1n\E_{\nu_0}\big[\langle x^*,X_s\rangle\langle x^*,X_0\rangle\big] =\partial_h\E_h\big[\langle x^*,X_s\rangle/n\big]\big|_{h=0}, $$ where $\E_h$ starts from the product tilt $\prod_i e^{hx^*_i\sigma_i}/(2\cosh h)$.

Proof. Cauchy–Schwarz. The identity for $\chi_n$ is the derivative of the tilted product law at $h=0$. ∎

So weak recovery on $[s_\ast,T]$ follows from $\chi_n(s)\ge c\sqrt n$ there, which gives $\E\,m(X_U)^2\ge c^2(1-s_\ast/T)$.

  • Not circular. $\chi_n$ is a two-time correlation under one fixed initial law, and for fixed $s$ it is local (its tree version is $\chi$). The signed Route T average, by contrast, was a Dynkin identity of the evolving law.
  • No iteration. Every $s$ refers to the same product start, so no block gain has to be iterated under a correlated law.

What is left

  • (T0) Reduction. Lemma 17. Done.
  • (T1) Tree growth. $\chi(s)\ge c\,e^{\gamma s}$ for $s\ge s_1$ with $\gamma\gt0$ throughout the window, or the same for the two-time correlation with $y(s_0)$. Moderate. It concerns one infinite-volume dynamics with no $n$, and the terms $\E_0[\sigma_o(s)\sigma_v(0)]$ are nonnegative by attractiveness. It needs a non-perturbative tool, such as a positive test vector for the linear hierarchy of odd-set responses, or a monotone comparison. Heuristic support: the $\omega$-weighted susceptibility of the free tree measure, $\sum_r(d\omega\omega_\beta)^r$, is infinite exactly when the informative condition holds, and that measure is extremal exactly when $\Lambda_\beta\lt1$. First step: simulate $\chi$ on a large Poisson tree and match its exponent to the escape rate measured on the SBM.
  • (T2) Transfer to logarithmic times. $\chi_n(s)\ge c\sqrt n$ at $s\sim\log n/(2\gamma)$. Hard: this is the real obstacle. It needs dependence over distances of order $\log n/\log(d\omega\omega_\beta)$, which is more than the tree-like radius $\tfrac12\log_d n$, so cycles matter, the same difficulty as in every proof at Kesten–Stigum. Realistic version: use locality up to $s_0=\epsilon\log n$, which gives a non-degenerate projection of size $n^{-1/2+\epsilon'}$ on the informative mode. Then amplify to order one with a spectral or linearized argument for $y$. That second phase is the windowed drift inequality restricted to typical configurations in the linear regime. It is weaker than requiring it for all $x$, but it still needs the configuration to be locally relaxed around its projection.
  • (T3) Persistence. $\chi_n(s)\ge c\sqrt n$ on all of $[s_\ast,T]$, i.e. the escape sign does not flip before $T$; the measured $\chi_n$ plateaus once $\E\,m^2$ saturates. Moderate. This is a near-equilibrium metastability statement around the informative state of $\mu_\beta$, where the signal is macroscopic. The bad configurations with $|y|\ge z$ and $m\approx0$ matter only if the trajectory reaches them, and along every recorded trajectory $|m|/|y|\approx0.5$–$0.9$.

Caveats: one graph per setting, a few hundred runs, standard errors of about 0.05 on the correlations; at $\lambda=1.3$, $\E\,m^2$ has not saturated by $s=500$.

The response route: overview

Everything above (WPI, flow LP, stopped duals, Theorem 12) certifies a statement for every starting state $x\in D$. Every failure so far had the same source: traps, adversarial stopping, and cold states that no static statistic separates from pinned ones. Theorem 12 itself survives numerically down to $\lambda=1.1$, but with $c\approx7\cdot10^{-4}$. That forces $T\approx10^6$ sweeps, and the certificate is fragile across graphs.

The simulations show what actually happens from the uniform start:

  • the overlap starts at the start's own fluctuation, of order $n^{-1/2}$;
  • it grows exponentially at a rate $\gamma$ that can be computed from the graph;
  • it saturates near $m_{\rm eq}$.

The response route proves this mechanism directly. It uses only three things: the law of one start, linear statistics, and one fixed time. Read backwards, the chain is:

  1. Theorem 14. A macroscopic response at one time gives weak recovery at that time.
  2. Theorem 15. A residual bound (RB) plus an entry condition give a macroscopic response at the escape time $s_*\approx\log n/(2\gamma)$.
  3. Theorem 16. On a tree paramagnet, the linear part of the generator is $-D_qH(t)$, the Bethe Hessian of Proposition 10. So the window of Proposition 10 is exactly where the linear dynamics has one unstable direction.
  4. Lemma 17, Theorem 18, Lemma 19. With memory, the linear model still has exactly one unstable direction. A comparison of the cold response with that model gives (RB).

What is open is the comparison (Hypothesis C) and some static inputs. All other steps are proved.

Weak recovery from the response

Setup. $L$ is the heat-bath generator for $\mu_\beta$, $P_s=e^{sL}$, time $s$ is in sweeps, and $X_0$ is uniform on $\{\pm1\}^n$. For a fixed vector $v\in\mathbb R^n$ (chosen in the next entry), define:

  • the score $Z_0:=\langle v,X_0\rangle$;
  • the response vector $r_s(i):=\E\big[Z_0\,\sigma_i(X_s)\big]$.

Since $\E[X_0X_0^\top]=I$, we have $r_0=v$ and $\E Z_0^2=\|v\|_2^2$. Also $\E[m(X_s)Z_0]=\langle x^*,r_s\rangle/n$.

Theorem 14 (fixed-time recovery). If for some time $s_*$ and some $c\gt0$ $$ \frac{\langle x^*,r_{s_*}\rangle}{n}\ \ge\ c\,\|v\|_2 ,\tag{G} $$ then $\E\,m(X_{s_*})^2\ge c^2$ and $\mathbb P\big(|m(X_{s_*})|\ge c/\sqrt2\big)\ge c^2/2$.

Proof. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $\E[m(X_{s_*})Z_0]^2\le\E\,m(X_{s_*})^2\,\E Z_0^2$, and the left side is $\big(\langle x^*,r_{s_*}\rangle/n\big)^2\ge c^2\|v\|_2^2$. For the tail bound, apply $\E w\le\mathbb P(w\ge\E w/2)+\E w/2$ to $w=m^2\in[0,1]$, as in Corollary 13. $\square$

Why test against a score. By symmetry $\E\,m(X_s)=0$, so the second moment is a genuinely nonlinear functional of the trajectory. Testing against $Z_0$ turns it into a first moment of linear observables. That first moment obeys an evolution equation, closed up to a residual (next entries). The only loss is the factor $\operatorname{corr}(Z_0,m_s)^2$, and all we need is that this factor does not depend on $n$.

Why a fixed time. Lemma 8 and Theorem 12 control time averages, hence the random output time $U$. Cauchy–Schwarz holds at every fixed $s$, so no averaging is needed. A random time can still be used later as a cheap substitute for persistence.

Caveats.

  • The pointwise analogue of (G) is false: there are configurations with $|m|\ll|\langle v,\sigma\rangle|/n$. Only the averaged response is aligned.
  • Theorem 14 gives recovery with constant probability. Upgrading to high probability needs $|m|$ to concentrate after saturation. That is seen numerically but not proved.
  • Past $s_*$, $\operatorname{corr}(Z_0,m_s)$ decays because the sign of $m$ mixes. So statements at much later times must bound $|m|$ directly.

Escape: from a residual bound to (G)

Static input (S). $M=-D_e\bar H$, where $D_e$ is a positive diagonal matrix: the mobilities, possibly corrected for memory. $\bar H$ is symmetric with exactly one negative eigenvalue. Put $W=D_e^{-1}$. Then $WM=-\bar H$ is symmetric, so $M$ is self-adjoint in $\langle x,y\rangle_W=x^\top Wy$ and has:

  • exactly one positive eigenvalue $\gamma$, with eigenvector $v$ normalized by $v^\top Wv=1$;
  • all other eigenvalues $\le-\mu_2\le0$.

Let $\Pi=vv^\top W$ and $Q=I-\Pi$. Decompose $$ r_s=\alpha_sv+p_s,\qquad \alpha_s=\langle v,r_s\rangle_W,\qquad p_s=Qr_s,\qquad u_s=\|p_s\|_W/\alpha_s, $$ and define the residual $\rho_s:=r_s'-Mr_s$: what the true response does, minus what the linear model predicts. The alignment constants are $$ \kappa_W=\frac{\langle x^*,v\rangle}{\sqrt n},\qquad s_x=\frac{\|QD_ex^*\|_W}{\sqrt n}\le1 . $$

Theorem 15 (escape). Fix $z\in(0,1)$ and let $s_*$ be the first time with $\alpha_s\ge z\sqrt n$. Assume:

  • (E) $\alpha_{s_0}\ge a_0\gt0$ and $u_{s_0}\le u_0$;
  • (RB) for $s\in[s_0,s_*]$, $$ \langle v,\rho_s\rangle_W\ \ge\ -\theta_\parallel\,\alpha_s,\qquad \|Q\rho_s\|_W\ \le\ \theta_\perp\,\alpha_s ,\tag{RB} $$ with $g_-:=\gamma-\theta_\parallel\gt0$.

Then:

  • $s_*\le s_0+\log(z\sqrt n/a_0)/g_-$;
  • $u_s\le\bar u:=\max\{u_0,\ \theta_\perp/(g_-+\mu_2)\}$ on $[s_0,s_*]$;
  • $\langle x^*,r_{s_*}\rangle/n\ \ge\ z\,(\kappa_W-s_x\bar u)$.

So (G) holds with $c=z(\kappa_W-s_x\bar u)/\|v\|_2$ whenever $\kappa_W\gt s_x\bar u$.

Proof. Since $Mv=\gamma v$ and $M$ commutes with $Q$:

  • $\alpha'=\gamma\alpha+\langle v,\rho\rangle_W\ge g_-\alpha$, which gives the first bullet.
  • $p'=Mp+Q\rho$, so $D^+\|p\|_W\le-\mu_2\|p\|_W+\theta_\perp\alpha$.
  • Hence $D^+u\le\theta_\perp-(g_-+\mu_2)u$, which gives the second bullet.

For the third, $\langle x^*,r\rangle=\langle D_ex^*,r\rangle_W=\alpha\langle x^*,v\rangle+\langle QD_ex^*,p\rangle_W\ge\alpha\sqrt n(\kappa_W-s_xu)$. At $s_*$ we have $\alpha_{s_*}=z\sqrt n$. $\square$

Why this form.

  • The $W$-geometry. $M$ is self-adjoint there, so its semigroup is controlled with constant 1. In the Euclidean norm, high-degree stars make it non-contractive.
  • Relative errors. The residual is measured against $\alpha_s$. It is allowed to grow with the signal, and it must: it is itself a linear response.
  • No bulk gap. Claim 11 shows there is none. The argument needs only $g_-+\mu_2\gt0$, and $\mu_2\ge0$ is automatic from the eigenvalue count.
  • The $\log n$. The aligned score has $r_0=v$, so $\alpha_0=1$ and $u_0=0$. The overlap needs $\alpha\sim\sqrt n$: the start's $n^{-1/2}$ fluctuation is amplified by $\sqrt n$.

Why the score $\langle v,X_0\rangle$. It starts the response exactly on the unstable mode. The natural alternative $Z_0=n\,m(X_0)$ starts it at $x^*$, which is mostly in the stable modes. The data confirm the difference: at $\lambda=1.1$ the old score gives $\operatorname{corr}\approx0$, and the aligned score gives $0.10$–$0.18$.

Caveat: (RB) fails during burn-in. The product start is not locally paramagnetic; the measured residual ratio is $+13$ to $+49$ at $s=0$. So entry is at $s_0=O(\log\log n)$, not at $0$.

Where $M$ comes from: the generator on a tree paramagnet

Theorem 16 (generator projection). Let $\mu$ be the free Ising measure on a finite forest with edge correlation $t$, and let:

  • $C=\E_\mu[\sigma\sigma^\top]$;
  • $H=C^{-1}$;
  • $q_i=1-\E_\mu\tanh^2h_i$ and $D_q=\operatorname{diag}(q_i)$;
  • $\zeta:=L\sigma+D_qH\sigma$.

Then:

  1. $\E_\mu[\zeta\sigma^\top]=0$.
  2. $H=H(t)=I+\frac{t^2}{1-t^2}D-\frac{t}{1-t^2}A$.
  3. $\E_\mu[\zeta\zeta^\top]=\operatorname{diag}\big(q_i(1-q_iH_{ii})\big)$, and $\zeta_i\equiv0$ at vertices of total degree $\le2$.

Proof of 1 and 2. The heat-bath Dirichlet form is $\mathcal E(f,g)=\sum_k\E[\operatorname{Cov}_k(f,g)]$, where $\operatorname{Cov}_k$ is the covariance under resampling site $k$. So $\E[\sigma_jL\sigma_i]=-\mathcal E(\sigma_i,\sigma_j)=-q_i\delta_{ij}$, and therefore $\E[\zeta_i\sigma_j]=-q_i\delta_{ij}+q_i(HC)_{ij}=0$. This step holds for any reversible measure. On a tree $C_{ij}=t^{\operatorname{dist}(i,j)}$, whose inverse is $H(t)$. Item 3 is in the memory note. $\square$

So $-D_qH\sigma$ is the $L^2(\mu)$-projection of $L\sigma$ onto linear observables. Taking $\E[Z_0\,\cdot\,]$ gives $$ r_s'=-D_qH\,r_s+\E[Z_0\,\zeta(X_s)] . $$ The linear model is this equation without the last term.

Why this is the right linear model. The matrix it produces is the Bethe Hessian of Proposition 10. By Ihara–Bass, the window $\sqrt d\lt1/t\lt d\omega$ is exactly where $H(t)$ has two negative directions, the uniform one and the informative one. On the SBM put $$ \bar H=H(t)+c'(uu^\top-I/n),\qquad c'=-n\beta J_n . $$ By the matrix determinant lemma, $\bar H$ has exactly one negative eigenvalue iff $1+c'u^\top H(t)^{-1}u\lt0$. The measured value is $-0.82$ to $-0.85$. So the unstable direction of $M$ is the informative non-backtracking direction, the vector a spectral algorithm would use. Glauber is noisy power iteration on $M$.

Data.

  • The formula for $q$ matches measured mobilities within $0.01$ at every degree.
  • Theorem 16 holds on the measured $\nu_s$ to about 3 digits.
  • Predicted growth rates $0.0135/0.0098/0.0061/0.0027$ against measured $0.0113/0.0107/0.0056/0.0047$.
  • The response's ratio to $x^*$ tends to $\kappa$, and its uniform component is about $0.01$.

Memory, and what (RB) reduces to

The residual is uncorrelated with spins at equal times, but not with their past. Let $\mathsf Q$ project off the single spins in $L^2(\mu)$, put $L_\perp=\mathsf QL\mathsf Q$, and define the memory kernel $\mathcal C(w)_{ij}=\langle e^{wL_\perp}\zeta_i,\zeta_j\rangle_\mu$.

Lemma 17 (exact memory equation, on a forest). For any start, $$ r_s'=-D_qHr_s+\int_0^s\mathcal C(w)Hr_{s-w}\,dw+a(s),\qquad a_i(s)=\E[Z_0\,(e^{sL_\perp}\zeta_i)(X_0)] . $$ $a\equiv0$ for a stationary start, but not for the cold start.

Theorem 18 (one unstable mode with memory). Write $\mathcal C(w)=R^*e^{-wB_\perp}R$, and assume passivity: $$ \mathcal K=\begin{pmatrix}D_q&-R^*\\-R&B_\perp\end{pmatrix}\succ0 . $$ On every finite forest, $\mathcal K$ is the Dirichlet form of $-L$ on odd functions, so passivity holds there. If $\bar H$ has exactly one negative eigenvalue, then the augmented generator $$ \mathcal G=-\mathcal K\operatorname{diag}(\bar H,I) $$ has exactly one positive eigenvalue $g$ and all its others are $\le0$. After a $C\log\log n$ burn-in, its solutions satisfy (RB), with constants controlled by $C_W=\|D_e^{-1/2}D_qD_e^{-1/2}\|^{1/2}$. No small-memory or bulk-gap assumption is needed.

Lemma 19 (transfer). Suppose that on $[s_0,s_*]$ the cold response is $r_s=r^{\rm lin}_s+e_s$, where $r^{\rm lin}$ solves the linear model and $$ |\langle v,e_s\rangle_W|\le\eta\,\alpha^{\rm lin}_s,\qquad |\langle v,e_s'-Me_s\rangle_W|\le d_\parallel\alpha^{\rm lin}_s,\qquad \|Q(e_s'-Me_s)\|_W\le d_\perp\alpha^{\rm lin}_s ,\tag{C} $$ with $\eta\lt1$. Then (RB) holds with $\theta_\parallel=(\varepsilon\gamma+d_\parallel)/(1-\eta)$ and $\theta_\perp=(\gamma\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)/\sqrt{1-2\varepsilon}+d_\perp)/(1-\eta)$.

Hypothesis C (cold comparison) is the statement that (C) holds with $n$-independent constants. This is the open core of the argument.

Why it cannot be an exact identity. On the SBM graph, $C_{\mu_\beta}^{-1}\succ0$. In the true equilibrium frame there is no unstable mode at all: all of the growth sits in the cold source $a(s)$. Comparing with the paramagnet is therefore a nonstationary local-equilibrium statement. It has to hold uniformly up to $s_*\sim\log n$, for a signed, two-time observable.

Data (exact tree computations).

  • Off-diagonal memory is about $10^{-3}$ of the diagonal.
  • On stars, $\widehat{\mathcal C}_{kk}/q_k$ saturates near $0.23$ as $k\to\infty$, so the passivity margin is $\ge0.77$ at every degree.
  • Depth roughly doubles the memory. On a degree-4 root, $\widehat{\mathcal C}/q$ goes from $0.016$ with leaves to $0.031$ with degree-4 neighbours.
  • Measured on the SBM, memory slows growth by $\theta_\parallel\approx-(0.12$–$0.31)\gamma$. Star values alone predict $-5$ to $-6\%$.

So the passivity margin uniform in degree on the Galton–Watson tree is not yet settled. It is measurable through $\widehat{\mathcal C}(g)=D_q-(C\widehat G(g)^{-1}-g)C$, where $\widehat G$ is the Laplace transform of the stationary spin autocorrelation.

The static input (S)

Setting. $G\sim\mathrm{SBM}(n,a/n,b/n)$ with two balanced communities, $d=(a+b)/2\gt1$, $\omega=(a-b)/(a+b)$ and $\lambda=d\omega^2\gt1$. $A$ is the adjacency matrix, $D$ the degree matrix, $B$ the non-backtracking matrix, and $u=\mathbf 1/\sqrt n$. For $t\in(0,1)$ put $r=1/t$ and $$ H(t)=I+\tfrac{t^2}{1-t^2}D-\tfrac{t}{1-t^2}A=\frac{\tilde H(r)}{r^2-1},\qquad \tilde H(r)=(r^2-1)I-rA+D, $$ $$ \bar H=H(t)+c'uu^\top,\qquad c'\ge0 . $$ The self-term $-c'I/n$ of the non-edge field is left out of $\bar H$ and kept in the residual. It contributes at most $c'(1+\bar u)/n$ to $\theta_\parallel$ and to $\theta_\perp$.

Proposition S. Fix $\varepsilon\gt0$ and suppose $\sqrt d+\varepsilon\le r\le d\omega-\varepsilon$, which is the window of Proposition 10.

  1. Algebra. Let $D_e\succ0$ be diagonal, and suppose $\bar H$ is nonsingular with exactly one negative eigenvalue. Then $M=-D_e\bar H$ is self-adjoint in $W=D_e^{-1}$. It has exactly one positive eigenvalue $\gamma$, and all its other eigenvalues are $\le-\mu_2\lt0$. Moreover, for every $y$ with $y^\top\bar Hy\lt0$, $$ \gamma\ \ge\ \frac{-y^\top\bar Hy}{y^\top D_e^{-1}y}. \tag{S.1} $$
  2. Bethe Hessian. With probability $1-o(1)$, $H(t)$ is nonsingular and has exactly two negative eigenvalues.
  3. Non-edge field. On that event, $\bar H$ has one or two negative eigenvalues, and it has exactly one if and only if $$ 1+c'\,u^\top H(t)^{-1}u\ \lt\ 0 .\tag{S.2} $$

So (S) holds, with probability $1-o(1)$, as soon as the scalar criterion (S.2) does.

Proof of (a). $WM=-\bar H$ is symmetric. $M=D_e^{1/2}\big(-D_e^{1/2}\bar HD_e^{1/2}\big)D_e^{-1/2}$ is similar to $S:=-D_e^{1/2}\bar HD_e^{1/2}$, and $S$ is congruent to $-\bar H$. By Sylvester's law of inertia, $S$ has exactly one positive eigenvalue and $n-1$ negative ones. If $Se_1=\gamma e_1$, then $v=D_e^{1/2}e_1$ satisfies $Mv=\gamma v$ and $v^\top Wv=1$. For (S.1), set $x=D_e^{-1/2}y$: then $\gamma\ge x^\top Sx/|x|^2=-y^\top\bar Hy/y^\top D_e^{-1}y$. $\square$

Proof of (b). Three ingredients.

  1. Ihara–Bass. For $r\gt1$, $$ \det(I-B/r)=(1-r^{-2})^{|E|-n}\,r^{-2n}\det\tilde H(r). $$ So $\tilde H(r)$ is singular exactly when $r$ is an eigenvalue of $B$.
  2. Non-backtracking spectrum (Bordenave–Lelarge–Massoulié, Thm. 4). With probability $1-o(1)$, $B$ has eigenvalues $d+o(1)$ and $d\omega+o(1)$, and every other eigenvalue has modulus at most $\sqrt d+o(1)$. So no eigenvalue of $B$ lies in $[\sqrt d+\varepsilon,d\omega-\varepsilon]$. By 1, $\tilde H(r)$ is then nonsingular on this whole interval. Since its eigenvalues are continuous in $r$ and never cross $0$ there, the number $N(r)$ of negative eigenvalues is constant on the interval.
  3. The count at the left end (Dong–McKenzie, Thm. 2.1, for every $d\gt1$; Stephan–Zhu, Thm. 3, for $d\ge2$). With probability $1-o(1)$, $N(\sqrt d+\varepsilon)=r_+$. Here $r_+$ is the number of eigenvalues of the signal matrix $Q=P\Pi$ exceeding $\sqrt d$. For the symmetric two-block model these eigenvalues are $d$ and $d\omega$, and both exceed $\sqrt d$ because $d\gt1$ and $\lambda\gt1$. So $r_+=2$.

Hence $N(r)=2$ on the whole window, and $H(t)$ has the same inertia as $\tilde H(r)$. $\square$

The upper bound $N(r)\le2$ alone needs only 1 and 2. It is Lemma 3.1 of Dong–McKenzie: $N(r)$ is at most the number of real eigenvalues of $B$ above $r$.

Proof of (c). $c'uu^\top\succeq0$ has rank one, so by interlacing $\lambda_k(H)\le\lambda_k(\bar H)\le\lambda_{k+1}(H)$. In particular $\lambda_1(\bar H)\le\lambda_2(H)\lt0$ and $\lambda_3(\bar H)\ge\lambda_3(H)\gt0$, so $\bar H$ has one or two negative eigenvalues. The sign of a nonsingular determinant is $(-1)^{\#\text{neg}}$, and $\det H\gt0$. By the matrix determinant lemma, $$ \det\bar H=\det H\,(1+c'u^\top H^{-1}u), $$ so there is exactly one negative eigenvalue if and only if (S.2) holds. $\square$

Why this is the right split. (b) is exactly the window of Proposition 10, now rigorous: inside the window the Bethe Hessian sees the uniform direction and the informative one, and nothing else. (c) is the balance condition. The non-edge field can remove at most one negative direction, and (S.2) says it removes the uniform one rather than leaving both. On a $d$-regular graph, $Hu=(1-\tfrac{td}{1+t})u$ exactly, so $$ u^\top H^{-1}u=\frac{1+t}{1+t-td}, $$ and (S.2) becomes $\tfrac{td}{1+t}-1\lt c'$. This is the "net linear coefficient of the uniform mode is below 1" condition of the temperature-window entry.

What is still open in (S)

  1. The scalar criterion (S.2) on the Poisson graph. Writing $\tilde B=\begin{pmatrix}A&I-D\\ I&0\end{pmatrix}$ for the reduced non-backtracking matrix, $$ u^\top\tilde H(r)^{-1}u=\tfrac1r\,\big[(rI-\tilde B)^{-1}\big]_{11}(u,u). $$ I checked this identity numerically to $10^{-15}$. It moves (S.2) onto the non-backtracking resolvent at a point between the bulk radius $\sqrt d$ and the outliers. The BLM decomposition of $B^\ell$ gives the outlier part explicitly, as a Galton–Watson quantity. The bulk part needs $\sum_k\tilde B^k/r^{k+1}$ on the bulk with $O(1)$ constants, but BLM only bound $\|B^\ell\|$ on the bulk for $\ell\asymp\log n$, with polylog losses. This is the missing estimate.
  2. Uniform constants. (a) gives $\mu_2\ge0$ for free, but Theorem 15 also needs $\gamma\ge c$ and $\kappa_W\ge c$, uniformly in $n$. By (S.1), $\gamma\ge c$ follows from a test vector $y$ with $-y^\top\bar Hy\ge c|y|^2$ and $\sum_iy_i^2/q_{e,i}\le C|y|^2$, i.e. one not concentrated on hubs. A natural candidate is the informative non-backtracking eigenvector pushed to vertices. By Stephan–Zhu (Lemma 17 there), if $\tilde B(x,y)^\top=\lambda(x,y)^\top$ then $\tilde H(\lambda)y=0$, and $$ y^\top\tilde H(r)y=(r-\lambda)\big(r|y|^2-y^\top(D-I)y/\lambda\big), $$ which is negative when $r\lambda\,|y|^2\gt y^\top(D-I)y$. Since $r\lambda\gt\sqrt d\cdot\sqrt d=d$, this reduces to a delocalisation statement: the degree-weighted mass of $y$ is at most about $d$. $\kappa_W$ is a similar overlap statement for the same vector.
  3. Finite-$n$ margins near threshold. The window has width $\min(d\omega-r,\ r-\sqrt d)$. At the balanced temperature this is $0.106$ at $\lambda=1.3$ and $0.036$ at $\lambda=1.1$. It is comparable to the finite-$n$ fluctuation of the informative outlier of $B$, so (b) can fail on a given graph of moderate size. This is what the two flagged graphs show (table below).
