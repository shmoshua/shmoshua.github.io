Wu topic: Glauber Dynamics paper: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20849 --- In this given setting, let $P$ be a Markov chain with stationary distribution $\pi$ on $\Omega$.
Definition (Locally Stationary Distribution)

A probability measure $\nu$ on $\Omega$ is $\varepsilon$-locally stationary w.r.t. $P$ if $$ \mathcal E_P(f,\log f):=\langle f,(I-P)\log f\rangle _\pi \le \varepsilon $$ where $f:= d\nu / d\pi$.

This locally stationary distribution framework then gives us a way to analyze the behaviors of Glauber dynamics by the following logic.
  1. Glauber dynamics gives a locally stationary distribution in $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ time.
  2. At a locally stationary distribution w.r.t Glauber, there is another dynamic for which there is a good transfer.
  3. With that given dynamic, the analysis of the objective is more tractable.
We will look at three different examples to see how they apply this logic. Before we dive into the examples, we show each part.

The Framework

Glauber Dynamics gives locally stationary distributions

Let $\nu_t:=\exp(-t(I-P))\nu$ and $f_t:= d\nu_t / d\pi$. Here, the most important identity is that the Dirichlet form is the derivative of KL divergence, i.e. $$ \dirchform{P}{f_t}{\log f_t} = -\frac{d}{dt}\KL(\nu_t\|\pi). $$ This is simply given by: $$ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt} \KL(\nu_t\|\pi) &= \frac{d}{dt}\langle f_t,\log f_t\rangle _\pi \\ &= -\langle (I-P)f_t,\log f_t\rangle _\pi + \langle 1,(I-P) f_t\rangle _\pi \\ &= -\langle (I-P)f_t,\log f_t\rangle _\pi + \langle (I-P)1, f_t\rangle _\pi \\ &= -\langle f_t,(I-P)\log f_t\rangle _\pi \\ &= - \dirchform{P}{f_t}{\log f_t} \end{aligned} $$ More concretely, for any distribution $\nu$ and $T > 0$, we have that: $$ \E_{t \sim[0,T]} \dirchform{P}{f}{\log f}\le \KL(\nu\|\pi) / T $$ This is given by $0 \le \KL(\nu_T\|\pi)=\KL(\nu\|\pi) - T\cdot \E_t\dirchform{P}{f_t}{\log f_t}$. Therefore, if we have a Gibbs distribution $\pi(x)\propto \exp(\frac{1}{2}x^\top J x)$, $$ \log \left(\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\right)=\log Z - \frac{1}{2}x^\top J x\le n\log 2 + \max_y |y^\top J y| $$ where $|y^\top J y|\le \|y\|_1 \|Jy\|_\infty = n\|Jy\|_\infty$. Therefore, if we run $T:=\frac{1}{\delta \varepsilon}\log (\frac{1}{\pi_{\min}})$ steps, then $\nu_t$ is $\varepsilon$-locally stationary with probability $1-\delta$.

Transfer via Measure Decomposition

Assume we have a measure decomposition of the form $\pi = \E_{z\sim\rho} [\pi_z]$. Notice that this can be represented using a Markov chain given as: $$ x \longrightarrow z|x \longrightarrow x'|z $$ More precisely, we can define the joint distribution $\mu(x,dz):=\rho(dz)\pi_z(x)$. Then, for any $x$:
  1. Sample $z\sim \mu(\cdot |x)$.
  2. Sample $x'\sim \pi_z$.
This is a reversible Markov chain with $\pi$ as the stationary distribution. Let $K$ denote this chain. Then, we get the following transfer lemma.
Lemma

For any function $f:\Omega \to \R_{> 0}$ and $\tau$ s.t. $f_\min > \exp(-\tau)$ or $f_\max < \exp(\tau)$, we have that: $$ \dirchform{K}{f}{\log f}\le O\left(\frac{\tau}{\delta}\right)\dirchform{P}{f}{\log f} $$ where $\delta:=\inf_z \rho_{\text{MLSI}}(\pi_z)$.

Note that the MLSI constant $\rho_{\text{MLSI}}(\pi)$ is given as the largest constant s.t. $$ \dirchform{\pi}{f}{\log f}\ge \rho_{\text{MLSI}}(\pi) \cdot \Ent_\pi[f] $$ where $\Ent_\pi[f]:= \E_\pi [f\log f] - \E_\pi f \cdot \log \E_\pi f$. I find this definition quite difficult to grasp. There are a few things:
  1. If $f=d\nu/ d\pi$, then $\Ent_\pi[f] = \E_\pi [f\log f] = \KL(\nu \|\pi)$
  2. $\Ent_\pi[c f] = c\E_\pi[f\log cf]-c\E_\pi f \cdot \log (c \cdot E_\pi f)=c \Ent_\pi[f]$
  3. If $f$ is unnormalized, i.e. $\E_\pi f=c$, then $f/c$ is normalized and $\Ent_\pi[f] = c\cdot \KL(\nu\|\pi)$ where $d\nu=\frac{1}{c}f d\pi$.
  4. Entropy is non-negative.
  5. Entropy is zero if and only if $\nu = \pi$.
  6. We want a large MLSI.

Boost Theorems

So we use the measure decomposition and hopefully the measure decomposition MC $K$ is better at analyzing the objective. We will see more of this in the following examples.

Weak Recovery in SBM

We define the setting of SBM as follows. $\lambda:=\varepsilon\sqrt{d}$ denote the signal-to-noise ratio. Then, we define $(\sigma, G)\sim \operatorname{SBM}(n,d,\lambda)$ as the process, where $\sigma\in \Omega$ sampled uniformly randomly, and the graph edges are sampled with: $$ \P(u\sim v | \sigma) =\frac{d}{n}\left(1+\varepsilon \sigma_u\sigma_v\right) $$
Theorem

There exists $\beta, c>0$ s.t. for all $\lambda$ large enough, we have that:

  1. run Glauber dynamics on $\mu_{\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{d}}(A-\frac{d}{n}11^\top)}$ from an arbitrary point $x_0\in \Omega$ for $T=\text{poly}(n)$.
With high probability, we have that for $t\sim [0,T]$: $$ \E_{x\sim \nu_t}|\langle \sigma, x\rangle | > cn $$

SBM as a spiked matrix and RGD

Let $v:= \sigma / \sqrt{n}$ and define the centered adjacent matrix $\bar A:= A - \E[A|\sigma]$. We have that: $\E[A|\sigma]=\frac{d}{n}11^\top +\frac{\lambda \sqrt{d}}{n}\sigma\sigma^\top$. Therefore, $$ M:=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{d}}\left(A-\frac{d}{n}11^\top\right) = \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\bar A + \lambda \sqrt{d} vv^\top \right) = \underbrace{\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{d}} \bar A}_{=:W} +\underbrace{\beta \lambda}_{=:\eta} vv^\top $$ Then, we have the measure decomposition $\mu_M=\E_{z\sim \rho}\mu_{W,z}$ where $z|x=(\eta \langle v, x\rangle + \sqrt{\eta} g)v$. The intuition is as follows: read the current overlap $\langle v,x \rangle$ and add noise of size $\sqrt{\eta}$ and resample the noise model pushed by that much.

SBM and the three steps

Notice that $\|My\|_\infty\le \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{d}}(\Delta_\max + d)$. Indeed, we have that: $$ |(My)_u|\le \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{d}}\left(|\partial u|+d\right) $$ W.h.p. we have that $\Delta_\max = O(\log n)$. Then we can assemble our result along the three steps as above as follows:
  1. Step 1: With $T$, Glauber outputs a $\tilde O(\frac{n}{\delta T})$-locally-stationary distribution with probability at least $1-\delta$.
  2. Step 2: It is given that for any $z$, $\rho_{\text{MLSI}}(\mu_{W,h})\ge n^{-1-o_d(1)}$. Hence, $\delta \ge n^{-1-o_d(1)}$. Further, from above, $\tau = O(n\log n)$. Therefore, $$ \dirchform{\text{RGD}}{f_t}{\log f_t}\le \tilde O( \frac{n^{3+o_d(1)}}{\delta T})\le o\left(n^{-2}\right) $$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ if $T=\Omega(n^{5+o_d(1)})$.
  3. Step 3: We have a two step argument. We define a proxy that RGD can only increase. However, local stationary implies that RGD should not be able to move it by much. Therefore, there cannot be much mass where RGD would strictly increase it.

Diving deeper into Step 3

Let define our potential function as $\psi:\Omega \to \R_{\ge 0},x\mapsto |\langle v,x\rangle|$. Our goal is to show that $\psi$ increases over an RGD step. However, at locally stationary distributions it can't increase by much.

First, to use local stationary, our potential function has to be bounded. Hence, we define: $\wt{\psi}(x):=\min\{\psi(x), 0.99\xi \sqrt n\}$ for some constant $\xi$. We have that for any bounded function:

Lemma

Let $\phi:\Omega \to \R$ be a bounded function and $\nu$ be $\varepsilon$-locally stationary. For any Markov chain $K$, $$ |\E_{\nu}[\phi]-\E_{K\nu}[\phi]|\le \|\phi\|_\infty \sqrt{\eps} $$

We have that: $$ \begin{aligned} |\E_{\nu}[\phi]-\E_{K\nu}[\phi]| &\le 2\|\phi\|_\infty \cdot d_{\text{TV}}(\nu, K\nu)\le \|\phi\|_\infty \sqrt{\dirchform{K}{f}{\log f}} \end{aligned} $$

∎

Therefore, we have that from Step 2, $$ |\E_{\nu_t}\wt{\psi}- \E_{K\nu_t}\wt{\psi}|\le 0.99 \xi \sqrt{n}\cdot o(n^{-1})= o(\xi / \sqrt{n}) $$ Now, we aim to show that RGD does improve $\wt{\psi}$. This eventually leads to a contradiction if $\E_{\nu_t}\wt{\psi}< 0.99\xi\sqrt{n}-2\delta$. Therefore, we can conclude that: $$ \E_{x\sim \nu_t}|\langle v, x\rangle|\ge \E_{\nu_t}\wt{\psi}\ge 0.99\xi \sqrt{n}-o(1) $$ This proves the desired statement.
